# O'Keeffe round 2: pages and the menu, one thing or two?

Five prototypes for one user test about a website's pages and the menu at the top of the site.

**This round's test uses three of them: the control, A and Q.** All three change the menu in its own sidebar, as WordPress does. B and F, which change it on the Pages screen, are kept for reference and still work, but aren't in the test. The test has a seventh task, adding a link to another website. It doesn't apply to B and F, which can't hold one.

A new block-theme site's header menu is automatic: it shows every published page, and new pages join it on their own. In WordPress today, the first time someone edits that menu it's converted to a list of links, and from then on it's a separate thing from the pages: new pages don't appear in it, and renaming a page doesn't rename its link.

The test asks two questions:

1. **Do people understand pages and the menu as one thing or two?** After changing the menu, can they say where a page went, what it's called, and whether the next new page will be in the menu?
2. **Can they change the menu?** Take a page out, reorder it, get a new page in, change what an item says, and add a link to another website.

It compares two things, crossed: what the menu *is* after you change it (converted to links, or still connected to the pages), and *where* you change it (on the Pages screen, or in the menu's own sidebar, opened by clicking the menu on the site). A fifth prototype, A, asks a narrower question: is it enough to keep new pages arriving in a menu of links, with nothing else about WordPress changed?

## The prototypes

| | Prototype | The menu after a change | Where the menu is changed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control | [WordPress today](control.html) | Converts to links | The menu's own sidebar, which follows the Navigation block's list in WordPress's editor. Click the menu in the preview and a **Navigation** sidebar opens. It lists one **Page List** row with the published pages under it. Those pages can't be changed: selecting one in the list, or starting to drag it, opens the **Detach Page List** dialog (Cancel, Detach; Enter confirms, Escape cancels). Selecting the Page List row, or clicking one of its pages in the menu on the site a second time (or double-clicking it), replaces the list with the Page List's settings, where there is a **Detach Page List** button. Detaching turns the Page List into links: today's published pages, in the same order and at the same levels. The Page List row has its own ⋮ menu, with Duplicate, Add before, Add after and **Remove Page List**, and there is a **+** below the list, so links can be added beside the Page List without detaching it (the menu then holds both, and the Page List still follows the pages), and the Page List can be removed, which takes all its pages out of the menu. Each row has its name and a ⋮ menu: **Move up**, **Move down**, **Add submenu link**, **Duplicate**, **Add before**, **Add after** and **Remove**. There are no handles: a row is dragged by its name. Selecting a link replaces the list with its settings, **Text** (the label, which changes the menu as you type) and **Link to** (the page and its address, or a typed address), with a back arrow to the list. **+** below the list, and Add before, Add after and Add submenu link, put a "Page link" row where the link will go and open **Search or type URL** under it: the site's published pages, newest first, narrowed as you type; a typed address, added with Enter; or **Create page**, which asks for a title, makes the page (published, unless Publish is turned off) and links to it. Dragging a row 40 pixels or more to the right of where the drag started puts it in the submenu of the row above, and 40 pixels or more to the left takes it out; otherwise it keeps its level. Nothing here takes effect on the site until **Save**. The Pages panel has no menu controls. |
| A | [Auto add](auto-add.html) | No Page List: links from the start, with new pages still added | The menu's own sidebar, opened the same way as the control's. There is no Page List and nothing to detach: the menu is ordinary links from the start, one for each published page, in the same order and at the same levels as every other prototype's header, each labelled with its page's title. The sidebar is the control's list of links, the same in every respect: rows dragged by their names, the ⋮ menu, a link's settings, **+** with Search or type URL and Create page, and Save. The one addition is a switch below the list labelled **Auto add pages**, on at the start, with nothing explaining it. While it's on, a page is added to the end of the menu as a link when it's published. Turning it off stops that, and turning it on again starts it for pages published from then on. The switch is part of the menu, so, like any change to the menu, switching it takes effect when it's saved. The Pages panel has no menu controls. |
| B | [Explained](explained.html) | Converts to links, and says so | The Pages screen. Every page has a marker saying whether it's in the menu: pale for "in your menu automatically", filled for in, a slashed outline for not in, dashed for "once published". Clicking a marker opens a popover with what that means and the actions: **Remove from menu** and **Rename in menu**. The first of either asks once ("you choose the menu's pages from now on … new pages won't be added on their own") and then converts. Rename in menu then asks for the new name; one message follows when that's done or cancelled, and its Undo takes back the conversion along with the name. After that, new pages are marked **New** and the popover offers **Add to menu** or **Keep it out**. Pages in the menu come first in the list, in menu order; drag a page by its handle, or use Move up and Move down in its ⋮ menu. |
| F | [Connected switches](switches.html) | Stays connected | The Pages screen. Every page has an **In menu** switch. Switching one off takes that page out of the menu and changes nothing else: no question, and the menu still follows the pages. The list is the menu's order: drag a page by its handle or use Move up and Move down, and child pages move with their parent. "Put under About" and "Take out from under About" change a page's parent. A new page arrives switched on; nothing in the panel says so beforehand, though a draft's row says "Shows in the menu once published". |
| Q | [Menu switches](menu-switches.html) | Stays connected | The menu's own sidebar, opened the same way as the control's. It lists the published pages as the menu's items, child pages under their parent, with no Page List row. Each has its name (a row is dragged by it), a **show/hide toggle** and a ⋮ menu (Move up, Move down). Switching a page off takes it out of the menu and changes nothing else. Moving a page changes page order, and the Pages panel's order with it; dragging it at least 40 pixels to the right of where the drag started makes it a child of the page above, and at least 40 pixels to the left takes it out, which changes its parent and so its address; otherwise it keeps the level it has (the same rule as the control's). Nothing else can be added and pages can't be removed from the list. Below the list, **Switch to advanced menu** asks first (Cancel, Switch), then turns the pages into links: the sidebar becomes the control's list of links, with **Hide** and **Show** in each ⋮ menu and an **Add new pages automatically** switch, on, below the list. Pages that were off become hidden links. Everything here is unsaved until **Save**; the pages that were moved are saved with the menu, and are listed with it in the save panel. The Pages panel has no menu controls. |

Two things follow from the model, and they are what the test is about:

- **Renaming.** While the menu is automatic or connected, a menu item's words are the page's title: rename the page and the menu follows, and there's no separate label. Once a menu has been converted to links, each link has its own label, copied from the page's title when it was converted (or when the link was added). After that, renaming the page doesn't change the link, and changing the link's label doesn't rename the page.
- **New pages.** An automatic or connected menu shows a page as soon as it's published, at its place in page order, which for a new page is the end. A menu that has been converted to links doesn't. Two menus of links are exceptions: Q's advanced menu adds a link for each newly published page until "Add new pages automatically" is turned off, and A's menu does the same until "Auto add pages" is turned off. A link added that way is a link like any other: it has its own label, it can be removed, and removing it doesn't bring it back.

In B and F, clicking a link in the preview's menu visits that page. In the control, A and Q, clicking the menu selects it, as it does in the editor, so pages are visited from the Pages panel; with the menu selected, a click on one of its links selects that link (or, in the control, the Page List that a page belongs to) and shows its settings. The sidebar is closed by its close button, by Escape, or by clicking anywhere else in the preview, and a reload leaves it as it was.

The control, A and Q share one sidebar and one way of saving. The list of links is the same in all three, apart from Hide, Show and "Add new pages automatically", which only Q's advanced menu has, and "Auto add pages", which only A has. A is the control after detaching, plus that switch: the same ⋮ menus, settings, Add link, save panel and log lines for the same actions.

### Saving, in the control, A and Q

These three change the menu inside WordPress's editor, so they have the editor's saving, and B and F, which are designs for the Pages screen, don't:

- **A change to the menu is unsaved until Save.** The preview shows it at once, as the editor's canvas does, but the site hasn't changed. **Save**, at the right of the top bar, is pale until there is something to save. It opens the save panel in the sidebar's place: Cancel and Save, "Are you ready to save?", "There is 1 site change waiting to be saved.", and a ticked **Navigation** under **Navigation Menus** (in Q, each moved page under **Pages** too). Saving shows "Site updated."
- **Undo and Redo** are two arrows in the top bar, and Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y (Cmd on a Mac). They go back and forward through every change to the menu, as many steps as there are, including across a save: undoing after saving leaves the undone change waiting to be saved again. A change to the menu shows no message.
- **The Pages panel is not part of that.** Add page, Rename, Publish and Move to trash apply at once in every prototype, with a message and its Undo. Ctrl+Z straight after one of them takes that back; otherwise it's the editor's Undo.
- **The tasks count once the change is saved**, in the notes panel and in the completion code. The code also says what was on screen but not saved.

In B and F every change applies at once, with the message's Undo.

## What's the same in all five

Anything that differs beyond the table above, and the saving described above, is a bug.

- **The site**: Rosa's Bakery, an invented bakery. Its pages, in page order: Home; About, with child pages Our story and Team; Bread and cakes; Catering; Visit us; Thank you for your order; and Holiday orders, a draft. The header menu starts automatic, so it reads Home, About (Our story, Team), Bread and cakes, Catering, Visit us, Thank you for your order. There's no footer menu.
- **The shell**: a top bar (the site's icon, "Pages", "Rosa's Bakery"; in the control, A and Q also Undo, Redo and Save), the Pages panel on the left, the site as visitors see it on the right, and a bar along the bottom with **Finished**. Nothing in the participant's view names the prototype or switches to another one, and there's no Reset there.
- **The preview** shows the header menu, the current page's title and placeholder content, and "Draft: only you can see this page" on a draft. It updates straight away. Visitors don't see drafts or hidden links, so the menu doesn't show them. Under the label "The site as visitors see it" is the page's address.
- **The Pages panel's basics**: the pages as a tree, a Draft badge, **Add page** (a title, then Save as draft or Publish), and a ⋮ menu on each row with **Rename**, View on site, Publish (on a draft) and Move to trash (not on Home). Move to trash asks first, with the same words in every prototype and nothing about the menu: "Move "Catering" to trash?", "Visitors won't be able to see this page any more.", Cancel and Move to trash. Clicking a page's title shows it in the preview, whether or not it's in the menu. The panel doesn't count the pages in the menu, since that would answer the first task.
- **Undo of what the Pages panel does**: Add page, Rename, Publish and Move to trash each show a message at the bottom with Undo; in B and F so does every change to the menu. The message stays for 12 seconds, and longer while the pointer is over it or the keyboard is in it. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on a Mac) does the same as the button and still works after the message has gone. Undo is one level: it takes back the last change, until the next change is made. The shortcut is left alone while typing in a field or while a dialog is open, and the message is never shown with a dialog open. Undo closes anything that's open first.
- **Reordering** is by drag and by Move up and Move down wherever a prototype lets things be reordered. In B and F a row has a handle, which also takes the up and down arrow keys, and a move shows ""Catering" moved". In the control, A and Q a row is dragged by its name, as in the editor. A drag let go of outside its list, or ended with Escape, moves nothing.
- **Dialogs** close with Cancel, Escape or a click outside, and a double-click on the button that opens one doesn't close it again. Escape only ever closes the topmost thing.
- **The tasks, the log, the Finished button and the completion code.**
- **The browser tab's title**, "Rosa's Bakery · Pages". The address still names the prototype (`control.html`, `auto-add.html`, `explained.html`, `switches.html`, `menu-switches.html`); hide it or use a link shortener if that matters for your test.
- **A reload keeps everything.** The site, the log and the counts are kept for the browser tab, so an accidental reload, or a testing tool reopening the link, doesn't start the participant again. A new tab starts fresh. Each prototype keeps its own.

The prototypes are for desktop browsers, from 1024 to 1500 pixels wide.

## Facilitator notes

Add `?notes` or `#notes` to the address (`switches.html?notes`) for a panel on the right that participants shouldn't see. It has:

- the prototype's letter and a line describing it;
- the seven tasks. Tasks 2 to 7 are ticked, with the time, when the site reaches the result, and each shows where it stands now. In the control, A and Q that is the saved site: a task that is done on screen but not saved isn't ticked, and says "On screen, not saved" in amber. Task 1 is a question, so it's never ticked for you. You can tick and untick any of them yourself;
- the counts that go into the completion code, and what the menu is now (in the control, A and Q: the saved menu, how many changes are unsaved, saves, and save panels closed without saving; in A, whether Auto add pages is on, and when it was turned off);
- a log of everything, with times since the page opened (or since Reset), and **Copy log** to paste it into your notes;
- Control | A | B | F | Q links to switch prototype, keeping the panel open, and **Reset**, which puts the site back and clears the log, the counts and what was kept for the tab.

The tasks aren't shown to participants: they read them in the testing tool, or you read them out.

The log records every page viewed in the preview and where from; every page added, published, renamed (the old and new title) and moved to trash; every Undo of a Pages panel change (and, in B and F, of any change), with what it undid and whether it was the button or the keyboard; every page taken out of the menu or put in, and how (switch, popover, remove, toggle); every reorder, with how (drag, Move up/down, or in B and F the arrow keys) and the places it moved from and to; each convert or switch-to-advanced dialog shown, confirmed or cancelled, and what set it off; every change to a link's label, with the old and new text and what the page is still called; each task reached, straight after the action that reached it; and Finished, with the code. It records the Add page, Rename, Move to trash and "Rename in menu" dialogs being opened and cancelled, and how (Cancel, Escape, a click outside); drags that moved nothing (let go without moving, cancelled with Escape, dropped outside the list), which aren't counted as reorders; and "Page reloaded" each time the page is loaded again in the same tab, with a line for any dialog the reload closed, so that dialogs shown, confirmed and cancelled add up. A reload while "Have you done every task?" is open says so, and isn't counted as turning back. In B it also records each marker popover opened. In the control, A and Q it records the Navigation sidebar opening and closing, and how (which menu item was clicked, the close button, Escape, a click elsewhere in the preview); the Page List row or a link being selected; each toggle; links added (and whether at the end of the menu or in a submenu), hidden, shown and removed; Add block opened, Back, and each block added with it; Link to opened, changed or closed; each row collapsed or expanded in the list, and how (chevron, ← or →); "Add new pages automatically" (Q) or "Auto add pages" (A) being switched; and reorders in the sidebar, with the level as well as the place ("from place 4 at the top level to place 1 under About"). Clicks on things that look as if they should do something and don't are recorded as "tried to …": selecting a page under the control's Page List (which opens the Detach Page List dialog), clicking a page's name in Q's list, trying to drag a page under the control's Page List, Move up on the first item or Move down on the last, Add submenu link where it's unavailable, the arrow keys on a handle at either end of a list (B and F), pressing Enter in Search or type URL on something that isn't an address, and clicking a page in the header menu of Q's pages menu once the menu is selected. In the control, A and Q it also records every click on Save and what the save panel listed; the panel saved (and anything unticked and left unsaved) or closed without saving, and how; each Undo and Redo, by the top bar or the keyboard, with the change it took back or put back, and whether that brought the menu back to what was saved; Undo, Redo or Save tried with nothing to do; each Add link opened (from +, Add before, Add after or Add submenu link) and where the "Page link" row was, links added from a suggestion, to a typed address, or with Create page, and Add link closed without adding, with how (Escape, the same control, a click elsewhere in the sidebar, the Pages panel, the preview or the top bar, Undo (which then undoes nothing else and isn't counted), Save, closing the sidebar, or a reload); the Page List removed or duplicated; a click in the header menu that selected a link or the Page List, and whether it was the second click of the double-click that opened the sidebar; a Text being typed when the page was reloaded, logged as renamed once it's loaded again; "Finished with 2 unsaved menu changes"; and, on a reload, how many unsaved changes were kept and whether the save panel was open.

## Running a test

Each prototype opens in a desktop browser with no sign-in. Use Reset in the notes panel between participants, or a new tab.

### Who

People who run, or are about to make, a small site: a business, a club, a portfolio. Not developers or theme authors, and not people who already know how WordPress menus work. Five or six per prototype finds the big problems.

### Design

One prototype per participant, since the first one teaches how the menu works and that carries over to the next. Change the order you run them in from day to day.

**Don't say "navigation", "link", "convert", "detach" or "automatic" before they do.** The tasks say what Rosa wants, not where to do it. Tasks 4 and 6 have a question to ask *before* the person looks at the site: ask it, then let them look.

**In the control, A and Q a change to the menu counts only once it's saved**, as in WordPress. Don't mention Save. Watch whether people save, when (after each change, or once at the end), and what they make of the save panel. If someone finishes without saving, the notes panel shows the tasks as "On screen, not saved" and the code has the dotted task part and a `w` above 0. In a moderated session, once they've said they're finished, ask: "Are those changes on the website now?"

Start with: "This is the Pages screen of a website for a bakery, Rosa's Bakery. On the right is the website as visitors see it. Rosa has asked you to make a few changes. Please think aloud as you go. There are no wrong answers; we're testing the design, not you."

### Tasks

1. "Without clicking anything: which of these pages can a visitor get to from the top of the website?"
   Then: **"How many pages does this site have, and how many are in the menu?"**
   *Watch for:* whether they read the menu in the preview or the list of pages; whether they count the child pages under About; whether they think the draft is in the menu. In B, whether the markers are read; in F, the switches.
2. "The Thank you for your order page is only for people who've just ordered. Take it out of the menu, without deleting it."
   Then: **"Where is that page now? Can a visitor still get to it?"**
   *Watch for:* where they look first, the page or the menu on the site. In the control, A and Q, whether they find that the menu can be clicked. In the control, what they make of the Page List row and the pages under it, how they get to the Detach Page List dialog (the log says which), and whether they read "new pages will no longer be added automatically". The control has two other ways to do this, both logged: Remove Page List, which takes every page out of the menu, and adding links beside the Page List. In B, how they read the confirm. Whether anyone reaches for Move to trash.
3. "Rosa wants Catering to come straight after Home in the menu."
   Then: **"Did anything else change order anywhere?"**
   *Watch for:* drag or Move up; in F and Q the list of pages changes order with the menu, and in the control it doesn't: do they notice, and does it match what they expected? In the control, A and Q, a drag only puts Catering under another item if it's moved 40 pixels or more to the right; the log says when that happens, and Undo puts it back. (WordPress itself nests far more readily: see below.) In Q the move also reorders the Pages panel at once, though it isn't saved yet.
4. "Rosa is starting to sell gift cards. Make a Gift cards page and publish it."
   Ask before they look at the site: **"Is the new page in the menu?"** Then: "Rosa wants it there. Make sure it is."
   *Watch for:* what they predict, and why. In the control and in B after a conversion the page isn't in the menu: how long until they see that, and can they add it? In the control, A and Q's advanced menu the page can also be made from the menu itself, with Create page in Add link; then the page is published at once and its link waits to be saved. In the control before detaching, the Page List shows the new page by itself. In B, does the popover that opens answer the question? In F and Q it's already there: do they look for something to do anyway? In A it's there too, as long as Auto add pages is on: do they connect that with the switch, and has anyone turned the switch off by then?
5. "In the menu, 'Visit us' should say 'Find us'."
   Then: **"What is the page itself called now?"**
   *Watch for:* whether they rename the page or look for the menu item's own text. Where the menu is links, renaming the page doesn't change the menu: do they notice, and what do they try next? In the control, a page renamed before the menu is detached does rename the menu item, and the link is then made with the new name. In Q's pages menu, clicking a page's name in the sidebar does nothing; the log records the try. Whether their answer about the page's name is right. In A the menu is links from the start, so renaming the page never changes it: only the link's Text does, and the code then reads `nl`. If the page was renamed as well, it reads `np`, with task 5 still 0 unless the link says Find us.
6. "Holiday orders is ready. Publish it."
   Ask before they look at the site: **"Will it be in the menu?"**
   *Watch for:* whether the answer follows from what they've seen so far. This is the same question as task 4, after more experience. In A the answer depends on the switch: the notes panel shows whether it's on.
7. "Rosa's Bakery has an Instagram page at instagram.com/rosasbakery. Add it to the menu at the top so visitors can find it there."
   Ask before they look at the site: **"If you made a new page now, would it be in the menu?"** (Yes, automatically · Only if I add it · Not sure)
   *Watch for:* in every arm the link is added with **+** below the list in the sidebar (or an item's Add before or Add after), by typing the address into Search or type URL and pressing Enter or clicking ↵ (Apply) in the field; an address without `https://` is accepted, and the link's label is the address as typed, less any `https://` (renaming it doesn't matter for the task). A link's Link to can also be changed to the address afterwards, which counts too. In the control, whether people add the link beside the Page List (the menu then holds both, and the code's `m` part reads `mp`) or detach first (`ml`), and, if they detach, whether they notice that new pages stop arriving afterwards. In A this should be the easy case. In Q the pages menu has no way to add a link: watch whether people find **Switch to advanced menu** (its dialog's trigger is logged; the code reads `mv`), and whether they think new pages still arrive afterwards, which they do. It counts once it's saved.

### What to watch for

- **The predictions in tasks 4 and 6.** This is the main measure of the first question. Compare what people say with what the prototype then does, per prototype. A right answer with a reason ("because I took one out, it stopped adding them") is worth more than a right guess.
- **Task 5, and the answer to "What is the page itself called now?"** The `n` part of the code says which was changed. Renaming the page is a fine answer where the menu is connected; where it's links, watch whether the person can tell that the page and the link now have different names.

- **Time to the first change to the menu** (the `f` part), and in the control, A and Q, how long before the menu on the site is clicked at all.

- **Saving, in the control, A and Q.** Whether people notice Save at all, whether the save panel stops them, and whether they leave with changes unsaved (the `w` and `v` parts, and the dotted task digits).
- **Task 7 in Q, and the control's route to it.** Whether the pages menu stops people, or they find Switch to advanced menu; in the control, beside the Page List or detach first (the `m` part). Compare the answer to the question before task 7 with what each prototype does afterwards.
- **Words.** Note what people call the menu ("the tabs", "the top bar", "the links") and whether "in your menu", "Navigation", "Page List" and "Detach" mean anything to them.

### What would favour each

- **Connected (F and Q)**: predictions in tasks 4 and 6 are right more often than in the control and B, task 5 is done by renaming the page without confusion, and nobody is surprised that reordering the menu reorders the pages.
- **Converting (the control and B)**: people want the menu and the pages to be separate, for example a menu label that differs from the page's title, or a menu order that differs from page order, and they understand that new pages need adding.
- **B over the control**: the confirm and the markers lead to right predictions where the control's Detach Page List dialog doesn't.
- **A over the control**: A differs from the control in two ways, not one: there is no Page List and no Detach step, and the switch keeps new pages arriving. So a gap on tasks 2 and 3 comes from the missing step, and only tasks 4 and 6 show what the switch does. In A's favour: predictions in tasks 4 and 6 are right and the new pages are where people expect, while the menu is still changed the way it is in WordPress. If people in A do as well on those tasks as in F and Q, keeping new pages arriving may be enough by itself.
- **A not being enough**: people leave Auto add pages alone and still can't say whether a new page will be in the menu; they turn it off without knowing what it does; or they rename the page in task 5 and don't see that the menu still says Visit us, which the connected prototypes avoid.
- **The Pages screen (B and F) over the sidebar (the control, A and Q)**: tasks 2 and 3 are started sooner and finished more often. Saving is part of this comparison, not a flaw in it: B and F are designs in which a change applies at once, and the sidebar arms are WordPress's editor, where it waits for Save. A participant who does everything in a sidebar arm and never saves has not changed the site, and the code says so.
- **The sidebar over the Pages screen**: people go to the menu on the site first and find what they need there, or say that changing the menu from a list of pages is odd.

## How it maps to WordPress

- **The automatic menu** is a Navigation block whose menu holds one Page List block, which is what a new block theme's header falls back to. Page List shows every published page in page order (`menu_order`), child pages as submenus.
- **Converting to links** is the Page List's own Detach step, which replaces it with a Page Link block for each page. Each link stores its own label.
- **What the control was checked against.** A running editor: WordPress 7.1.4 with the Gutenberg plugin 24.4.1, Twenty Twenty-Five and this site's pages, in WordPress Playground, driven step by step with the header's Navigation block selected. Collapsing rows and Add block were checked later, in WordPress 7.1.5 with Gutenberg 24.4.2, the same way. Everything in this list was seen there unless it says "from source", which means read in `@wordpress/block-library` 12.1.0 and `@wordpress/block-editor` 20.0.0 and not seen running.
- **The Page List step, as seen.** The first click on the menu on the canvas selects the Navigation block; a second click on a page selects the Page List, and further clicks do nothing more: a click on the canvas never opens the Detach dialog. In the sidebar's list, the Page List row has the pages under it, and clicking one of those opens the dialog at once. Clicking the Page List row replaces the list with the Page List's settings, with a back arrow, a Parent field and an outlined "Detach Page List" button. The dialog: title "Detach Page List", a close button, "This Navigation Menu displays your website's pages. Detaching will enable you to add, delete, or reorder pages. However, new pages will no longer be added automatically.", "Cancel" and "Detach"; the close button is labelled "Cancel" too. It takes the keyboard itself, Enter confirms it and Escape cancels it (seen when the control was checked again in the same versions); the control does the same, and so does Q's Switch to advanced menu dialog, which is also a ConfirmDialog in Quinn. The Page List row's ⋮, in order: Move up, Move down, Add submenu link (all three unavailable while it's alone), Duplicate, Add before, Add after, Remove Page List. Remove Page List asks nothing and leaves "This Navigation Menu is empty." Starting to drag a page under the Page List opens the dialog at once, before it's let go of, wherever it's then dropped (seen); so does the control. A double-click on a page in the menu on the canvas, with nothing selected, selects the Page List, as two clicks do (seen).
- **Links, as seen.** After detaching, each row is a name and a ⋮: Move up, Move down, Add submenu link, Duplicate, Add before, Add after, Remove followed by the name. Rows have no handles and drag whole. A row with rows under it (the Page List, a page with child pages under it, a link with a submenu) has a chevron before its name, pointing down: everything starts expanded. Clicking the chevron hides or shows the rows under it without selecting anything, ← and → on the row do the same, and the row's name says which (`aria-expanded`; the chevron is hidden from screen readers). The list forgets it: after selecting a row and going back, or a reload, everything is expanded again. A collapsed row dragged elsewhere takes its rows along and stays collapsed. Clicking a row replaces the list with the link's settings: Text, Link to (a button: the page's title, address and "Page" and "Published"; for a typed address, the address and "External link"), and more below. + (labelled "Add page"; and Add before, Add after, Add submenu link) puts a "Page link" row in the list and opens a popover: "Search or type URL", the site's pages newest first, then "Create page" and "Add block". A click on another row or on empty space in the sidebar, or the top bar's Undo, closes it and takes the "Page link" row away; Undo then undoes nothing else. A click on the canvas closes it but leaves an empty "Page link" row behind. Create page puts the keyboard on Back first. Typing searches; a typed address and Enter, or the ↵ (Apply) button inside the field, makes a link labelled with the address less any `http://` or `https://` (`https://www.instagram.com/rosasbakery/` gives "www.instagram.com/rosasbakery/"); Apply is unavailable until something is typed; Escape takes the "Page link" row away. **Add block**, after Create page, shows Back (which takes the keyboard), "Blocks" and six blocks: Page link (back to Search or type URL), Custom Link (the same, the row reading "Custom Link", without Create page; a typed address makes a link), Buttons (a Buttons block with an empty Button, drawn as a dark "Add text…" pill), Page List (a second Page List), Search (a search field and button), Social Icons (empty, drawn as a small outlined +), then Browse all. Create page asks for a Title (placeholder "No title") and has a Publish switch, on, with "Turn off to save as a draft. Drafts won't appear on your site until published.", then Cancel and Create page; the page is made at once, before any Save. A picked page's link is labelled with the page's title. Duplicate selects the copy.
- **Saving and undo, as seen.** No change to the menu shows a message. Undo and Redo are in the top bar (Ctrl+Z; Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z), many levels, and still work after a save, leaving unsaved changes. Save reads "Save" and is pale until there's something to save. It opens the save panel where the sidebar was: Cancel and Save, "Are you ready to save?", "There is 1 site change waiting to be saved.", and under "Navigation Menus" a ticked "Navigation". After saving: "Site updated." Typing in Text changes the menu as it's typed, and one Undo takes all of it back. That a moved page is listed under "Pages" in Q is worked out from the editor's name for pages, not seen: stock WordPress can't move pages from the menu, and Quinn's build wasn't run.
- **Where the control still departs from WordPress, and why.**
  - *The route in.* There is no site editor around it: no template part, block toolbar, breadcrumb, block inserter or Styles. The Pages panel sits beside the site, and the menu is reached by clicking it there. That was a given for this round. So the toolbar's own "Detach" button, on the canvas, is missing; the sidebar's button and the dialog are there.
  - *The sidebar is the list and the settings only.* WordPress's has tabs (list, settings, styles), a menu chooser beside the "Navigation" heading, and, in a link's settings, Open in new tab, Edit, View, Description and Rel attribute; in the Page List's, Parent and Advanced. None of these are here. Parent would change which pages the Page List shows, which the tasks don't allow for.
  - *Dragging is kinder.* In WordPress a row dropped on another with the pointer over its name becomes that row's child, with no sideways movement at all: dragging Catering straight up onto Home, held by its name, put it in Home's submenu. Here a row changes level only after 40 pixels of sideways movement, the same in the control, A and Q. Copying WordPress would have made task 3 fail for most people who drag, for a reason that isn't what the test is about.
  - *Two levels.* Links go two levels deep, so Add submenu link is unavailable on a link that's already in a submenu; in WordPress it isn't.
  - *Link to.* In WordPress it's a button; what clicking it shows wasn't seen. Here it opens a field with the link's address (a page link's is its path, such as `/catering`) and ↵ (Apply) inside it: Enter or Apply changes where the link goes, if what's typed is an address, and keeps its text; Escape leaves it as it was. A page link changed this way becomes a link to that address. It's logged, and a change to Rosa's Instagram counts for task 7.
  - *Add block.* There is no Browse all (it opens the editor's full inserter, which isn't here). In a submenu it offers Page link and Custom Link only; what WordPress offers there wasn't seen. The other blocks have no settings: selecting one shows its description, and selecting the Button inside Buttons selects the Buttons. They can be moved, duplicated and removed, can't have a submenu or go in one, and count for the tasks only by being in the menu (Search between Home and Catering means Catering isn't straight after Home). In A and Q's advanced menu a Page List added this way shows every published page and can be detached, with WordPress's Detach Page List dialog (in Q too, not Switch to advanced menu).
  - *Collapsing rows.* Q's pages menu has chevrons too (on a page with child pages), on the assumption that Quinn's list is the editor's list view; Quinn's build wasn't run. There ← and → work on the row's switch and ⋮, and nothing carries `aria-expanded`, since the page's name isn't a control. → on an expanded row doesn't move the keyboard to its ⋮, as WordPress's does. Dropping a row onto a collapsed parent (moved 40 pixels to the right) puts it last in that submenu; WordPress's wasn't seen.
  - *⋮ is always shown* on a row; WordPress shows it on hover, focus or selection.
  - *Add link.* The popover opens under the "Page link" row, not beside the sidebar. Any click outside it, including on the site, closes it and takes the "Page link" row away; in WordPress a click on the canvas leaves an empty "Page link" row in the menu. It suggests pages only (WordPress also finds posts, categories and tags). A typed address that starts with `javascript:` isn't accepted; WordPress accepts it. The "Select page" placeholder isn't drawn in the menu on the site. Suggested pages are in the order they were made, newest first; WordPress orders by when they were last changed.
  - *Create page in A and Q's advanced menu.* While the saved menu adds new pages (A's Auto add pages on, Q's advanced menu with Add new pages automatically on), a page published with Create page is added as any published page is: one link, at the end of the menu (not where the "Page link" row was), already in the saved menu, with nothing to save. So task 4 doesn't depend on whether the page was made in the Pages panel or in Add link. With the setting off, or in the control, the link goes where the "Page link" row was and waits for Save.
  - *A double-click on the menu on the site* opens the sidebar and then does what a second click does (in the control, selects the Page List; in A, the link). For half a second after a click on the menu opens the sidebar, other clicks in the preview are ignored (a click on another item of the menu is logged as ignored), because the sidebar's arrival moves the preview under the pointer. The same goes for the second click of a double-click on a ⋮ item or on a row whose settings replace the list.
  - *New pages go last.* A page made here goes to the end of page order, as the round's site was specified. In WordPress a new page has order 0, so a Page List showed it first.
  - *An emptied Text is put back* when the field is left. WordPress lets a link have no text.
  - *Undo is one step for one action.* WordPress's is finer in places (adding a link is more than one step).
  - *The save panel's ticks.* Unticking Navigation and saving leaves the menu unsaved, as in WordPress. In Q, page order is saved as one thing: saving any moved page saves where all of them are.
  - *"Site updated."* has no "View site" link, since there's nowhere to go.
  - *After removing a link* the keyboard goes to the next row; in WordPress it's lost.
  - *The Pages panel's changes aren't the editor's.* They apply at once and aren't in the editor's Undo. Where one touches the menu (a trashed page's links go; a published page is added by auto-add) it's made to the saved menu and the unsaved one alike. The saved menu's auto-add setting is the one that decides, since that's the menu the site acts on: switching it off and publishing a page before saving still adds the page.
- **WordPress 7.0's wording.** The block library that WordPress 7.0 ships (9.40.2, from source) words the Page List step differently: a notice in the Page List's settings with the same sentence but "Editing it will enable you…", an "Edit" button beside it, and an "Edit Page List" dialog with "Cancel" and "Edit". The control uses the Gutenberg plugin's current wording, as seen running.
- **A** is a Navigation block whose menu was made as links, with no Page List in it, plus one setting saved on the menu that adds a link for each page when it's first published, in the way of the `auto_add_pages` setting that [Quinn](../../quinn/)'s advanced menus have. Here it comes without Quinn's pages menu, toggles or Hide: everything else is the control after detaching, departures and all. The classic Menus screen has a setting of the same name, "Auto add pages: Automatically add new top-level pages to this menu"; the prototype uses the name without the sentence. A page published while the switch is off isn't added later, and a link that was removed isn't put back.
- **B** is the same conversion, started from the Pages screen and explained in the prototype's own words. "New" would need WordPress to store which pages have been seen since the menu was converted.
- **F** is a Page List that leaves some pages out, which needs a setting on the block for the pages that are off. Its order is page order, and "Put under" changes a page's parent, and so its address.
- **Q** simulates [Quinn](../../quinn/), a Gutenberg pull request: a menu that is only a Page List lists its pages in the Navigation block's sidebar with a toggle each, and "Switch to advanced menu" turns them into links on a menu that still adds new pages. Following Quinn, switching a parent page off in Q hides its child pages too, though their toggles stay on; in F they move up to the top level, as they did in O'Keeffe's first round. Following Quinn again, nothing warns that moving a page under another changes its address. New pages are always made at the top level here, so an advanced menu adds each one at its end. The words of the Switch to advanced menu dialog and its "Switch" button are the prototype's, written from Quinn's description. As in F, Home can't be put under another page and nothing can be put under Home. Changing a row's level is by drag only (40 pixels or more to the right or left, as above), so it can't be done from the keyboard; Move up and Move down move a row within its level. Quinn is built on the Navigation block's list, so Q's rows are dragged by their names and its advanced menu is the control's list of links. A page published while a switch to an advanced menu is unsaved isn't added as a link, since the saved menu is still a pages menu, which shows it anyway.
- **Drafts** aren't shown in the preview's menu in any prototype, since the preview is the site as visitors see it and WordPress doesn't output a link to an unpublished page.

## Running it

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and nothing loaded from elsewhere. `app.js` and `style.css` in this folder are round 2's own copy of the O'Keeffe engine, so the first round's prototypes in the folder above are unchanged. Each page sets `window.PROTOTYPE = { arm }` to `control`, `auto-add`, `explained`, `switches` or `menu-switches`. Serve the repository root with any static server and open `okeeffe/round-2/`.
