# O'Keeffe round 2: pages and the menu, one thing or two?

Five prototypes for one user test about a website's pages and the menu at the top of the site.

**This round's test uses three of them: the control, A and Q.** All three change the menu in its own sidebar, as WordPress does. B and F, which change it on the Pages screen, are kept for reference and still work, but aren't in the test. The test has a seventh task, adding a link to another website. It doesn't apply to B and F, which can't hold one.

A new block-theme site's header menu is automatic: it shows every published page, and new pages join it on their own. In WordPress today, the first time someone edits that menu it's converted to a list of links, and from then on it's a separate thing from the pages: new pages don't appear in it, and renaming a page doesn't rename its link.

The test asks two questions:

1. **Do people understand pages and the menu as one thing or two?** After changing the menu, can they say where a page went, what it's called, and whether the next new page will be in the menu?
2. **Can they change the menu?** Take a page out, reorder it, get a new page in, change what an item says, and add a link to another website.

It compares two things, crossed: what the menu *is* after you change it (converted to links, or still connected to the pages), and *where* you change it (on the Pages screen, or in the menu's own sidebar, opened by clicking the menu on the site). A fifth prototype, A, asks a narrower question: is it enough to keep new pages arriving in a menu of links, with nothing else about WordPress changed?

Two things follow from the model, and they are what the test is about:

- **Renaming.** While the menu is automatic or connected, a menu item's words are the page's title: rename the page and the menu follows, and there's no separate label. Once a menu has been converted to links, each link has its own label, copied from the page's title when it was converted (or when the link was added). After that, renaming the page doesn't change the link, and changing the link's label doesn't rename the page.
- **New pages.** An automatic or connected menu shows a page as soon as it's published, at its place in page order, which for a new page is the end. A menu that has been converted to links doesn't. Two menus of links are exceptions: Q's advanced menu adds a link for each new page until "Add new pages automatically" is turned off, and A's menu does the same until "Auto add pages" is turned off: as soon as it's made, if it's made as a draft (the link is shown as a draft on the site, and visitors see it once the page is published), or when it's published. A link added that way is a link like any other: it has its own label, it can be removed, and removing it doesn't bring it back.

In B and F, clicking a link in the preview's menu visits that page. In the control, A and Q, clicking the menu selects it, as it does in the editor, so pages are visited from the Pages screen (Edit or View); with the menu selected, a click on one of its links selects that link (or, in the control, the Page List that a page belongs to) and shows its settings. The sidebar is closed by its close button, by Escape, or by clicking anywhere else in the preview, and a reload leaves it as it was.

The control, A and Q share one sidebar and one way of saving. The list of links is the same in all three, apart from Hide, Show and "Add new pages automatically", which only Q's advanced menu has, and "Auto add pages", which only A has. A is the control after detaching, plus that switch and the drafts it adds (Holiday orders' link at the start, and links to drafts drawn as drafts on the site): the same ⋮ menus, settings, Add link, save panel and log lines for the same actions.

### Saving, in the control, A and Q

These three change the menu inside WordPress's editor, so they have the editor's saving, and B and F, which are designs for the Pages screen, don't:

- **A change to the menu is unsaved until Save.** The preview shows it at once, as the editor's canvas does, but the site hasn't changed. **Save**, at the right of the top bar, is pale until there is something to save. It opens the save panel in the sidebar's place: Cancel and Save, "Are you ready to save?", "There is 1 site change waiting to be saved.", and a ticked **Navigation** under **Navigation Menus** (in Q, each moved page under **Pages** too). Saving shows "Site updated."
- **Undo and Redo** are two arrows in the top bar, and Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y (Cmd on a Mac). They go back and forward through every change to the menu, as many steps as there are, including across a save: undoing after saving leaves the undone change waiting to be saved again. A change to the menu shows no message.
- **What's done to pages is not part of that.** Add page, Rename, Publish and Move to trash (in the control, A and Q: Add Page, Rename…, the top bar's Publish and Trash…) apply at once in every prototype, with a message and its Undo. Ctrl+Z straight after one of them takes that back; otherwise it's the editor's Undo.
- **The tasks count once the change is saved**, in the notes panel and in the completion code. The code also says what was on screen but not saved.

In B and F every change applies at once, with the message's Undo.

## What's the same in all five

Anything that differs beyond the table above, and the saving described above, is a bug.

- **The site**: Rosa's Bakery, an invented bakery. Its pages, in page order: Home; About, with child pages Our story and Team; Bread and cakes; Catering; Visit us; Thank you for your order; and Holiday orders, a draft. The header menu starts automatic, so it reads Home, About (Our story, Team), Bread and cakes, Catering, Visit us, Thank you for your order. There's no footer menu.
- **The shell**: a top bar and a bar along the bottom with **Finished**. Nothing in the participant's view names the prototype or switches to another one, and there's no Reset there. In B and F, which are designs for the Pages screen, the top bar has the site's icon, "Pages" and "Rosa's Bakery", the Pages panel is on the left and the site on the right, under the label "Your site". The control, A and Q have WordPress's site editor instead, the same in all three (see "The site editor" below): the top bar has the site's icon, "Rosa's Bakery", Undo, Redo and Save, and the pages are on the site editor's Pages screen.
- **The preview** shows the header menu, the current page's title and placeholder content, and "Draft: only you can see this page" on a draft. It updates straight away. The menu doesn't show hidden links or drafts, apart from Q's pages menu, whose drafts that are switched on are shown as drafts (see the table). Above it are the label "Your site", the same in every prototype, and the page's address.
- **The Pages panel's basics** (B and F): the pages as a tree, a Draft badge, **Add page** (a title, then Save as draft or Publish), and a ⋮ menu on each row with **Rename**, View on site, Publish (on a draft) and Move to trash (not on Home). Move to trash asks first, with nothing about the menu: "Move "Catering" to trash?", "Visitors won't be able to see this page any more.", Cancel and Move to trash. Clicking a page's title shows it in the preview, whether or not it's in the menu. The control, A and Q have WordPress's Pages screen instead (below). Neither counts the pages in the menu.
- **Undo of what's done to pages**: adding, renaming, publishing and trashing a page each show a message at the bottom with Undo; in B and F so does every change to the menu. The message stays for 12 seconds, and longer while the pointer is over it or the keyboard is in it. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on a Mac) does the same as the button and still works after the message has gone. Undo is one level: it takes back the last change, until the next change is made. The shortcut is left alone while typing in a field or while a dialog is open, and the message is never shown with a dialog open. Undo closes anything that's open first.
- **Reordering** is by drag and by Move up and Move down wherever a prototype lets things be reordered. In B and F a row has a handle, which also takes the up and down arrow keys, and a move shows ""Catering" moved". In the control, A and Q a row is dragged by its name, as in the editor. A drag let go of outside its list, or ended with Escape, moves nothing.
- **Dialogs** close with Cancel, Escape or a click outside, and a double-click on the button that opens one doesn't close it again. Escape only ever closes the topmost thing.
- **The tasks, the log, the Finished button and the completion code.**
- **The browser tab's title**, "Rosa's Bakery · Pages". The address still names the prototype (`control.html`, `auto-add.html`, `explained.html`, `switches.html`, `menu-switches.html`); hide it or use a link shortener if that matters for your test.
- **A reload keeps everything.** The site, the log and the counts are kept for the browser tab, so an accidental reload, or a testing tool reopening the link, doesn't start the participant again. A new tab starts fresh. Each prototype keeps its own.

### The site editor, in the control, A and Q

These three put the menu inside a simulation of WordPress's site editor, as it was seen running (see "How it maps to WordPress"), the same in all three:

- **Design.** A dark column on the left: a back arrow ("Go to the Dashboard", which does nothing but is logged), **Design**, "Customize the appearance of your website using the block editor.", and the entries **Identity, Styles, Pages, Navigation, Patterns, Templates**, in WordPress's order. The main area is the site, which is edited as before: click the header menu for its sidebar. At the column's foot, "✓ Saved", or, with something unsaved, a blue **Review N changes…** that opens the same save panel as Save.
- **Pages.** The column becomes **Pages**, with a back arrow to Design and the filters All Pages, Published, Scheduled, Drafts, Pending, Private and Trash. The main area becomes WordPress's Pages screen: a list column (**Pages**, a blue **Add Page**, Search, and a row per page with a grey square for its picture, the title, "Homepage" on Home, and its status, "Published" or "Draft", in WordPress's order: by title, A to Z, child pages straight after their parent) and, beside it, a preview of the selected page (the front page until a row is clicked). A row's **Edit**, or a click on the preview, goes back to Design with that page on the site. Its ⋮ (Actions) has WordPress's items in WordPress's order: **Edit, View, Trash…, Duplicate…, Rename…, Order…** and, on a published page other than Home, Set as homepage… and Set as posts page…; Duplicate… and the two Set as… are greyed (not part of the prototype; a click is logged). There's no Publish there, as in WordPress. Home has no Trash… (a departure: WordPress offers it, but the prototype has no site without its front page). **Add Page** asks for a title in WordPress's "Draft new: Page" dialog (Title, placeholder "No title"; Cancel, Create draft): the draft is made, and opens on the site. That's also how a draft such as Holiday orders is published: Edit, then the top bar's **Publish**.
- **Order…** is WordPress's dialog: "Order", "Determines the order of pages. Pages with the same order value are sorted alphabetically. Negative order values are supported.", a number field ORDER holding the page's number, Cancel and Save. Each page has the number WordPress gave the round's pages (Home 0, About 1, Our story 2, Team 3, Bread and cakes 4, Catering 5, Visit us 6, Thank you for your order 7, Holiday orders 8); page order is these numbers, then the titles, as in WordPress. Save applies it at once, like Rename, with "Order updated." and Undo; it isn't a menu change, so there's nothing to save and the editor's Undo doesn't take it back. Whatever follows page order follows it: the control's Page List, a Page List added with Add block, Q's pages menu, and so the menu on the site and task 3. As in WordPress, Catering 1 ties with About 1 and comes after it, so Catering straight after Home takes two numbers (Catering 1 and About 2), or a number below Home's (which puts it first). What's typed is taken as a whole number (0.5 is 0; nothing is 0). A new page goes last, after the highest number; moving pages in Q's pages menu numbers them afresh, in order.
- **The top bar's button** is WordPress's page editor's: while the page on the site is a draft and nothing else is waiting to be saved, "✓ Saved" and **Publish**, which publishes it at once; otherwise **Save**, which opens the save panel (pale with nothing to save). With a draft and unsaved menu changes it's Save, and the save panel lists only the menu; once that's saved, Publish is back and the page is still a draft. So the page and the menu are never saved together, as in WordPress. **Rename…** is WordPress's Rename dialog (Name; Cancel, Save); **Trash…** asks "Are you sure you want to move "Catering" to the trash?" (Cancel, Trash). Each keeps its message with Undo. The Trash filter lists pages in the trash, without actions.
- **Navigation.** The column becomes **Navigation**: "Navigation Menus are a curated collection of blocks that allow visitors to get around your site.", then the menu's list, the same list as the menu's sidebar (the same rows, toggles, ⋮ menus, Add link, settings, dragging and collapsing), editing the same menu, unsaved until Save or Review changes. It's a second way to change the menu. The main area is the site as a preview; a click on it goes back to Design.
- **Identity, Styles, Patterns, Templates** each open a screen of their own in the column that says it isn't part of this prototype, with a back arrow, and are logged.
- Opening any screen other than Design closes the menu's sidebar. A reload keeps the screen, the Pages screen's filter, search and selected row.

The prototypes are for desktop browsers, from 1024 to 1500 pixels wide.

## Facilitator notes

Add `?notes` or `#notes` to the address (`switches.html?notes`) for a panel on the right that participants shouldn't see. It has:

- the prototype's letter and a line describing it;
- the seven tasks. Tasks 2 to 7 are ticked, with the time, when the site reaches the result, and each shows where it stands now. In the control, A and Q that is the saved site: a task that is done on screen but not saved isn't ticked, and says "On screen, not saved" in amber. Task 1 is a question, so it's never ticked for you. You can tick and untick any of them yourself;
- the counts that go into the completion code, and what the menu is now (in the control, A and Q: the saved menu, how many changes are unsaved, saves, and save panels closed without saving; in A, whether Auto add pages is on, and when it was turned off);
- a log of everything, with times since the page opened (or since Reset), and **Copy log** to paste it into your notes;
- Control | A | B | F | Q links to switch prototype, keeping the panel open, and **Reset**, which puts the site back and clears the log, the counts and what was kept for the tab.

The tasks aren't shown to participants: they read them in the testing tool, or you read them out.

The log records every page viewed in the preview and where from; in the control, A and Q, every entry clicked in the site editor's navigation, each screen opened and left and how (the entry, a back arrow, Edit or View on the Pages screen, a click on the preview, Create draft), the Pages screen's filters and selected rows, greyed items tried, every Order… (the old and new number, the page's place before and after, and the header menu after), and, for each page renamed or trashed, that it was done on the Pages screen, and for each publish, that it was the top bar's Publish with the page on the site; every page added, published, renamed (the old and new title) and moved to trash; every Undo of a change to a page (and, in B and F, of any change), with what it undid and whether it was the button or the keyboard; every page taken out of the menu or put in, and how (switch, popover, remove, toggle); every reorder, with how (drag, Move up/down, or in B and F the arrow keys) and the places it moved from and to; each convert or switch-to-advanced dialog shown, confirmed or cancelled, and what set it off; every change to a link's label, with the old and new text and what the page is still called; each task reached, straight after the action that reached it; and Finished, with the code. In the control, A and Q it also records every click on Save and what the save panel listed; the panel saved (and anything unticked and left unsaved) or closed without saving, and how; each Undo and Redo, by the top bar or the keyboard, with the change it took back or put back, and whether that brought the menu back to what was saved; Undo, Redo or Save tried with nothing to do; each Add link opened (from +, Add before, Add after or Add submenu link) and where the "Page link" row was, links added from a suggestion, to a typed address, or with Create page, and Add link closed without adding, with how (Escape, the same control, a click elsewhere in the sidebar, the Pages panel, the preview or the top bar, Undo (which then undoes nothing else and isn't counted), Save, closing the sidebar, or a reload); the Page List removed or duplicated; a click in the header menu that selected a link or the Page List, and whether it was the second click of the double-click that opened the sidebar; a Text being typed when the page was reloaded, logged as renamed once it's loaded again; "Finished with 2 unsaved menu changes"; and, on a reload, how many unsaved changes were kept and whether the save panel was open.

| `d1.1` | Convert or switch-to-advanced dialogs shown, then confirmed, including the Detach Page List dialog of a Page List added with Add block. Always `d0.0` in F, which has no such dialog, and in A unless a Page List was added with Add block and its Detach was tried. |
| `f35` | Seconds until the first change to what the menu shows or its order, however it was made, saved or not. That includes taking a page out, moving one and changing a link's label, and also renaming, adding, publishing or trashing a page while the menu is automatic or connected, since the menu follows the pages then. `f0`: never. |
| `r1.2` | Reorders by drag, then by Move up and Move down, saved or not. In B and F the arrow keys on a handle count with Move up and Move down. A drag that moved nothing isn't counted. |
| `np`, `nl`, `n0` | How task 5 was done. `np`: the page was renamed, so its title is now "Find us". `nl`: only a link's label was changed. `n0`: neither. In the control, A and Q this is the saved site too. |
| `x0` | Pages moved to trash and not restored. |
| `u2.1` | Uses of Undo, then of Redo. Undo is the message's button, the top bar's arrow (control, A, Q) or Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z; Redo is the top bar's arrow or the keyboard. B and F have no Redo, so theirs is always `u2.0`. |
| `k3` | The prototype's own count. Control, A and Q: times the Navigation sidebar was opened. B: marker popovers opened by the person (the one that opens by itself after Add page isn't counted). F: switches flipped. |
| `o1` | Control, A and Q: times Order… on the Pages screen moved a page to another place in page order (a number changed without moving it isn't counted; the log has every use). Always `o0` in B and F. It stands beside `r`, which counts reorders in the menu. |
| `g40` | Control, A and Q: seconds from opening the page until the site editor's Pages screen was first opened, however it was opened. `g0`: never. Always `g0` in B and F, which have no such screen. |
| `y30` | Control, A and Q: seconds until the site editor's Navigation screen was first opened, `y0` if never: with `k` (the menu's sidebar opened from the site), it says whether and how the menu was found. Always `y0` in B and F. |
| `e0` | Times Finished was clicked and the person turned back at "Have you done every task?". |
| `w2` | Unsaved changes to the menu when Finished was clicked: the changes made since the last save, less any undone (or, after undoing past a save, the number undone). `w0` when the screen is what was saved, and always in B and F. |
| `v2.1` | Saves, then times the save panel was opened and closed without saving. Always `v0.0` in B and F. |
| `s412` | Seconds from opening the page to clicking Finished. |

The tasks are read from the site as it is when Finished is clicked (the saved site in the control, A and Q), not from the ticks. A change that was undone doesn't count towards the tasks or the `m` part, but the dialogs, reorders and switches that led to it stay counted. Finished asks the same question in every prototype and says nothing about unsaved changes. Every part starts with its own letter, apart from the prototype and the task digits, which are always first and second.

## Running a test

Each prototype opens in a desktop browser with no sign-in. Use Reset in the notes panel between participants, or a new tab.

### Who

People who run, or are about to make, a small site: a business, a club, a portfolio. Not developers or theme authors, and not people who already know how WordPress menus work. Five or six per prototype finds the big problems.

### Design

One prototype per participant, since the first one teaches how the menu works and that carries over to the next. Change the order you run them in from day to day.

**Don't say "navigation", "link", "convert", "detach" or "automatic" before they do.** The tasks say what Rosa wants, not where to do it. Tasks 4 and 6 each have a prediction to ask *before* the task starts: ask it, then read the task.

**In the control, A and Q a change to the menu counts only once it's saved**, as in WordPress. Don't mention Save. Watch whether people save, when (after each change, or once at the end), and what they make of the save panel. If someone finishes without saving, the notes panel shows the tasks as "On screen, not saved" and the code has the dotted task part and a `w` above 0. In a moderated session, once they've said they're finished, ask: "Are those changes on the website now?"

Start with, in the control, A and Q: "This is where the owner of a website for a bakery, Rosa's Bakery, makes changes to it. The website is on the right." In B and F: "This is the Pages screen of a website for a bakery, Rosa's Bakery. On the right is the website." Then: "Rosa has asked you to make a few changes. Please think aloud as you go. There are no wrong answers; we're testing the design, not you."

### Tasks

1. Look only: **"Without changing anything, find where you would change the menu at the top of the site."**
   Then: **"Where would you change it? How did you find it?"** (short text)
   *Watch for:* in the control, A and Q, whether they click the menu on the site (that opens its sidebar, which changes nothing) or go to the site editor's **Navigation** entry (the same list, in the left column); the times of both are in the log. In A, as in Q, the menu on the site already shows "Holiday orders (Draft)". In B and F, whether they look to the Pages screen. This task isn't scored.
2. "The Thank you for your order page is only for people who've just ordered. Take it out of the menu, without deleting it."
   Then: **"Where is that page now? Can a visitor still get to it?"**
   *Watch for:* where they look first, the page or the menu on the site. In the control, A and Q, whether they find that the menu can be clicked. In the control, what they make of the Page List row and the pages under it, how they get to the Detach Page List dialog (the log says which), and whether they read "new pages will no longer be added automatically". The control has two other ways to do this, both logged: Remove Page List, which takes every page out of the menu, and adding links beside the Page List. In B, how they read the confirm. Whether anyone reaches for Move to trash.
3. "Rosa wants Catering to come straight after Home in the menu."
   Then: **"Did anything else change order anywhere?"**
   *Watch for:* drag or Move up, or, in the control, A and Q, **Order…** on the Pages screen (the code's `o` part; in A it changes page order but not the menu of links); in F and Q the list of pages changes order with the menu, and in the control it doesn't: do they notice, and does it match what they expected? In the control, A and Q, a drag only puts Catering under another item if it's moved 40 pixels or more to the right; the log says when that happens, and Undo puts it back. (WordPress itself nests far more readily: see below.) In Q the move also changes page order at once, though it isn't saved yet; the Pages screen lists pages by title, as WordPress's does, so it doesn't show that.
4. Ask before they start the task: **"When you publish a new page, will it show in the menu by itself?"** (Yes, automatically · No, I'll have to add it · Not sure)
   Then the task: "Rosa is starting to sell gift cards. Make a Gift cards page and publish it." Then: "Rosa wants it there. Make sure it is."
   *Watch for:* in the control, A and Q, whether they find **Pages** in the site editor's navigation (the code's `g` part is the seconds until they first did, and the log says how they got there), and whether they find Publish in the top bar after Create draft. What they predict, and why. In the control and in B after a conversion the page isn't in the menu: how long until they see that, and can they add it? In the control, A and Q's advanced menu the page can also be made from the menu itself, with Create page in Add link; then the page is published at once and its link waits to be saved. In the control before detaching, the Page List shows the new page by itself. In B, does the popover that opens answer the question? In F and Q it's already there: do they look for something to do anyway? In Q a Gift cards page saved as a draft shows in the menu on the site as a draft; it counts only once it's published, and it's in the saved menu. In A it's there too, as long as Auto add pages is on: do they connect that with the switch, and has anyone turned the switch off by then? In A the link arrives when the draft is made (Create draft), drawn as "Gift cards (Draft)", before it's published: does that answer the prediction for them, and do they then think publishing is still needed? It counts only once it's published.
5. "In the menu, 'Visit us' should say 'Find us'."
   Then: **"What is the page itself called now?"**
   *Watch for:* whether they rename the page or look for the menu item's own text. Where the menu is links, renaming the page doesn't change the menu: do they notice, and what do they try next? In the control, a page renamed before the menu is detached does rename the menu item, and the link is then made with the new name. In Q's pages menu, clicking a page's name in the sidebar does nothing; the log records the try. Whether their answer about the page's name is right. In A the menu is links from the start, so renaming the page never changes it: only the link's Text does, and the code then reads `nl`. If the page was renamed as well, it reads `np`, with task 5 still 0 unless the link says Find us.
6. Ask after task 5's questions and before task 6: **"Rosa is going to add a new page next month. When she publishes it, will it appear in the menu by itself?"** (Yes, automatically · No, she'll have to add it · Not sure). It comes after the person's own changes to the menu, so it asks about the menu as they've left it, and nothing on screen answers it in any prototype.
   Then the task: "Holiday orders is ready. Publish it." In the control, A and Q it's on the Pages screen: Edit, then Publish in the top bar (there's no Publish in its ⋮, as in WordPress). Watch whether people find it there, and how long that takes.
   Then: **"Is Holiday orders in the menu now?"** (Yes · No · Not sure) and **"Why do you think that is?"** (short text)
   *Watch for:* whether the prediction follows from what they've seen and done so far; it's the same question as the one before task 4, after more experience. In A the answer depends on the switch: the notes panel shows whether it's on. In A too, Holiday orders has been in the menu on the site as "Holiday orders (Draft)" from the start (Auto add pages added it); publishing keeps that link, unless it was removed, in which case it stays out. In Q, Holiday orders was already shown in the menu on the site marked "(Draft)" before it was published, with its toggle on in the sidebar; if its toggle was switched off (and saved), publishing leaves it out. The task reaches 2 only once it's published and in the saved menu: being drawn as a draft doesn't count. The log says whether its toggle was on or off when it was published.
7. "Rosa's Bakery has an Instagram page at instagram.com/rosasbakery. Add it to the menu at the top so visitors can find it there."
   Ask before they look at the site: **"If you made a new page now, would it be in the menu?"** (Yes, automatically · Only if I add it · Not sure)
   *Watch for:* in every arm the link is added with **+** below the list in the sidebar (or an item's Add before or Add after), by typing the address into Search or type URL and pressing Enter or clicking ↵ (Apply) in the field; an address without `https://` is accepted, and the link's label is the address as typed, less any `https://` (renaming it doesn't matter for the task). A link's Link to can also be changed to the address afterwards, which counts too. In the control, whether people add the link beside the Page List (the menu then holds both, and the code's `m` part reads `mp`) or detach first (`ml`), and, if they detach, whether they notice that new pages stop arriving afterwards. In A this should be the easy case. In Q the pages menu has no way to add a link: watch whether people find **Switch to advanced menu** (its dialog's trigger is logged; the code reads `mv`), and whether they think new pages still arrive afterwards, which they do. It counts once it's saved.

Then click Finished.

### Questions to close with

- "What's the difference between a page and the menu?"
- "If you made a new page tomorrow, would it be in the menu?"

### What to watch for

- **The predictions before tasks 4 and 6.** This is the main measure of the first question. Compare what people say with what the prototype then does, per prototype. A right answer with a reason ("because I took one out, it stopped adding them") is worth more than a right guess. The prediction before task 6 is scored against the menu's real state at the end, not against task 6 (Holiday orders is a page that already exists, and in Q it was on screen as a draft): a new page joins the menu by itself if the saved menu still adds new pages, which the code's `m` part says (`ma`, and `mp` while the control's Page List is still there: yes; `ml` and `mr`: no; `mv`, with or without its `p`: yes; `mx`, with or without its `p`: no, Add new pages automatically or Auto add pages is off in the saved menu). Task 6's own follow-up, "Is Holiday orders in the menu now?", is checked against task 6's digit (2: yes).
- **Task 5, and the answer to "What is the page itself called now?"** The `n` part of the code says which was changed. Renaming the page is a fine answer where the menu is connected; where it's links, watch whether the person can tell that the page and the link now have different names.
- **Task 2 done with Move to trash**, and then Undo or not. The `x` part counts pages left in the trash.
- **Time to the first change to the menu** (the `f` part), and in the control, A and Q, how long before the menu on the site is clicked at all.
- **Dialogs confirmed without reading.** Ask what it said. The `d` part gives shown and confirmed.
- **Saving, in the control, A and Q.** Whether people notice Save at all, whether the save panel stops them, and whether they leave with changes unsaved (the `w` and `v` parts, and the dotted task digits).
- **Task 7 in Q, and the control's route to it.** Whether the pages menu stops people, or they find Switch to advanced menu; in the control, beside the Page List or detach first (the `m` part). Compare the answer to the question before task 7 with what each prototype does afterwards.
- **Words.** Note what people call the menu ("the tabs", "the top bar", "the links") and whether "in your menu", "Navigation", "Page List" and "Detach" mean anything to them.

### What would favour each

- **Connected (F and Q)**: predictions before tasks 4 and 6 are right more often than in the control and B, task 5 is done by renaming the page without confusion, and nobody is surprised that reordering the menu reorders the pages.
- **Converting (the control and B)**: people want the menu and the pages to be separate, for example a menu label that differs from the page's title, or a menu order that differs from page order, and they understand that new pages need adding.
- **B over the control**: the confirm and the markers lead to right predictions where the control's Detach Page List dialog doesn't.
- **A over the control**: A differs from the control in two ways, not one: there is no Page List and no Detach step, and the switch keeps new pages arriving. So a gap on tasks 2 and 3 comes from the missing step, and only tasks 4 and 6 show what the switch does. In A's favour: predictions before tasks 4 and 6 are right and the new pages are where people expect, while the menu is still changed the way it is in WordPress. If people in A do as well on those tasks as in F and Q, keeping new pages arriving may be enough by itself.
- **A not being enough**: people leave Auto add pages alone and still can't say whether a new page will be in the menu; they turn it off without knowing what it does; or they rename the page in task 5 and don't see that the menu still says Visit us, which the connected prototypes avoid.
- **The Pages screen (B and F) over the sidebar (the control, A and Q)**: tasks 2 and 3 are started sooner and finished more often. Saving is part of this comparison, not a flaw in it: B and F are designs in which a change applies at once, and the sidebar arms are WordPress's editor, where it waits for Save. A participant who does everything in a sidebar arm and never saves has not changed the site, and the code says so.
- **The sidebar over the Pages screen**: people go to the menu on the site first and find what they need there, or say that changing the menu from a list of pages is odd.

## How it maps to WordPress

- **The automatic menu** is a Navigation block whose menu holds one Page List block, which is what a new block theme's header falls back to. Page List shows every published page in page order (`menu_order`), child pages as submenus.
- **Converting to links** is the Page List's own Detach step, which replaces it with a Page Link block for each page. Each link stores its own label.
- **What the control was checked against.** A running editor: WordPress 7.1.4 with the Gutenberg plugin 24.4.1 (the site editor's navigation, Pages and Navigation screens later, in 7.1.5 with 24.4.2), Twenty Twenty-Five and this site's pages, in WordPress Playground, driven step by step with the header's Navigation block selected. Collapsing rows and Add block were checked later, in WordPress 7.1.5 with Gutenberg 24.4.2, the same way. Everything in this list was seen there unless it says "from source", which means read in `@wordpress/block-library` 12.1.0 and `@wordpress/block-editor` 20.0.0 and not seen running.
- **The Page List step, as seen.** The first click on the menu on the canvas selects the Navigation block; a second click on a page selects the Page List, and further clicks do nothing more: a click on the canvas never opens the Detach dialog. In the sidebar's list, the Page List row has the pages under it, and clicking one of those opens the dialog at once. Clicking the Page List row replaces the list with the Page List's settings, with a back arrow, a Parent field and an outlined "Detach Page List" button. The dialog: title "Detach Page List", a close button, "This Navigation Menu displays your website's pages. Detaching will enable you to add, delete, or reorder pages. However, new pages will no longer be added automatically.", "Cancel" and "Detach"; the close button is labelled "Cancel" too. It takes the keyboard itself, Enter confirms it and Escape cancels it (seen when the control was checked again in the same versions); the control does the same, and so does Q's Switch to advanced menu dialog, which is also a ConfirmDialog in Quinn. The Page List row's ⋮, in order: Move up, Move down, Add submenu link (all three unavailable while it's alone), Duplicate, Add before, Add after, Remove Page List. Remove Page List asks nothing and leaves "This Navigation Menu is empty." Starting to drag a page under the Page List opens the dialog at once, before it's let go of, wherever it's then dropped (seen); so does the control. A double-click on a page in the menu on the canvas, with nothing selected, selects the Page List, as two clicks do (seen).
- **Links, as seen.** After detaching, each row is a name and a ⋮: Move up, Move down, Add submenu link, Duplicate, Add before, Add after, Remove followed by the name. Rows have no handles and drag whole. A row with rows under it (the Page List, a page with child pages under it, a link with a submenu) has a chevron before its name, pointing down: everything starts expanded. Clicking the chevron hides or shows the rows under it without selecting anything, ← and → on the row do the same, and the row's name says which (`aria-expanded`; the chevron is hidden from screen readers). The list forgets it: after selecting a row and going back, or a reload, everything is expanded again. A collapsed row dragged elsewhere takes its rows along and stays collapsed. Clicking a row replaces the list with the link's settings: Text, Link to (a button: the page's title, address and "Page" and "Published"; for a typed address, the address and "External link"), and more below. + (labelled "Add page"; and Add before, Add after, Add submenu link) puts a "Page link" row in the list and opens a popover: "Search or type URL", the site's pages newest first, then "Create page" and "Add block". A click on another row or on empty space in the sidebar, or the top bar's Undo, closes it and takes the "Page link" row away; Undo then undoes nothing else. A click on the canvas closes it but leaves an empty "Page link" row behind. Create page puts the keyboard on Back first. Typing searches; a typed address and Enter, or the ↵ (Apply) button inside the field, makes a link labelled with the address less any `http://` or `https://` (`https://www.instagram.com/rosasbakery/` gives "www.instagram.com/rosasbakery/"); Apply is unavailable until something is typed; Escape takes the "Page link" row away. **Add block**, after Create page, shows Back (which takes the keyboard), "Blocks" and six blocks: Page link (back to Search or type URL), Custom Link (the same, the row reading "Custom Link", without Create page; a typed address makes a link), Buttons (a Buttons block with an empty Button, drawn as a dark "Add text…" pill), Page List (a second Page List), Search (a search field and button), Social Icons (empty, drawn as a small outlined +), then Browse all. Create page asks for a Title (placeholder "No title") and has a Publish switch, on, with "Turn off to save as a draft. Drafts won't appear on your site until published.", then Cancel and Create page; the page is made at once, before any Save. A picked page's link is labelled with the page's title. Duplicate selects the copy.
- **Saving and undo, as seen.** No change to the menu shows a message. Undo and Redo are in the top bar (Ctrl+Z; Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z), many levels, and still work after a save, leaving unsaved changes. Save reads "Save" and is pale until there's something to save. It opens the save panel where the sidebar was: Cancel and Save, "Are you ready to save?", "There is 1 site change waiting to be saved.", and under "Navigation Menus" a ticked "Navigation". After saving: "Site updated." Typing in Text changes the menu as it's typed, and one Undo takes all of it back. That a moved page is listed under "Pages" in Q is worked out from the editor's name for pages, not seen: stock WordPress can't move pages from the menu, and Quinn's build wasn't run.
- **Where the control still departs from WordPress, and why.**
  - *The route in.* The site editor's navigation, Pages screen and Navigation screen follow WordPress's (seen in 7.1.5 with Gutenberg 24.4.2), but on Design the main area is the site itself, editable, with the top bar's Undo, Redo and Save, and a click on the header menu opens its sidebar. In WordPress, Design's main area is a preview of the front page with no top bar; a click on it opens the editor on that page, where a click on the header selected the locked Header template part (with "Edit original"), not the menu, and the header's menu wasn't drawn at all. So in WordPress the menu is changed on the Navigation screen or by editing the Header template part; clicking the menu on the site is this round's given. There is no template part, block toolbar, breadcrumb, block inserter or Styles, so the toolbar's own "Detach" button is missing; the sidebar's button and the dialog are there.
  - *The Pages screen.* In WordPress, Edit opens the page in the editor, where the top bar's first button goes back to the Pages screen, and View opens the published page in a new tab; here both go to Design with the page on the site. The rows' pictures are grey squares; there are no filter, layout or view-options buttons; Search matches titles only; Duplicate…, Set as homepage… and Set as posts page… are greyed; Trash lists trashed pages without Restore or Delete; Home has no Trash… (WordPress offers it). An emptied Order… field is saved as 0: not confirmed in WordPress, where the emptied field showed 0 again before Save. WordPress gives a new page the number 0; here it gets the next number, so it goes last, as the round's site was specified. The status filters are filters, without WordPress's "Status includes" filter bar beyond a chip. The messages are the prototype's own, with Undo (WordPress's: '"Gift cards" successfully created.', "Page published."). On the Pages screen the message sits over the preview, clear of the list, so it doesn't cover its ⋮ buttons.
  - *The Navigation screen* shows the same list as the menu's sidebar. WordPress's leaves the Page List row out (its pages are listed at the top level), its ⋮ has Move up, Move down, Go to <page> and Remove, a click on a link's name does nothing, and the heading's ⋮ has Rename, Edit, Duplicate and Delete; here the rows, ⋮ menus and settings are the sidebar's, the heading has no ⋮, and in Q the toggles are there too (Quinn's own screen wasn't seen).
  - *Identity, Styles, Patterns and Templates* each open a screen that says it isn't part of the prototype; in WordPress, Identity and Styles keep the Design column.
  - *Opening another screen closes the menu's sidebar*, and the Pages screen keeps the site's preview beside the list rather than WordPress's iframe of the page.
  - *The sidebar is the list and the settings only.* WordPress's has tabs (list, settings, styles), a menu chooser beside the "Navigation" heading, and, in a link's settings, Open in new tab, Edit, View, Description and Rel attribute; in the Page List's, Parent and Advanced. None of these are here. Parent would change which pages the Page List shows, which the tasks don't allow for.
  - *Dragging is kinder.* In WordPress a row dropped on another with the pointer over its name becomes that row's child, with no sideways movement at all: dragging Catering straight up onto Home, held by its name, put it in Home's submenu. Here a row changes level only after 40 pixels of sideways movement, the same in the control, A and Q. Copying WordPress would have made task 3 fail for most people who drag, for a reason that isn't what the test is about.
  - *Two levels.* Links go two levels deep, so Add submenu link is unavailable on a link that's already in a submenu; in WordPress it isn't.
  - *Link to.* In WordPress it's a button; what clicking it shows wasn't seen. Here it opens a field with the link's address (a page link's is its path, such as `/catering`) and ↵ (Apply) inside it: Enter or Apply changes where the link goes, if what's typed is an address, and keeps its text; Escape leaves it as it was. A page link changed this way becomes a link to that address. It's logged, and a change to Rosa's Instagram counts for task 7.
  - *Add block.* There is no Browse all (it opens the editor's full inserter, which isn't here). In a submenu it offers Page link and Custom Link only; what WordPress offers there wasn't seen. The other blocks have no settings: selecting one shows its description, and selecting the Button inside Buttons selects the Buttons. They can be moved, duplicated and removed, can't have a submenu or go in one, and count for the tasks only by being in the menu (Search between Home and Catering means Catering isn't straight after Home). In A and Q's advanced menu a Page List added this way shows every published page and can be detached, with WordPress's Detach Page List dialog (in Q too, not Switch to advanced menu).
  - *Collapsing rows.* Q's pages menu has chevrons too (on a page with child pages), on the assumption that Quinn's list is the editor's list view; Quinn's build wasn't run. There ← and → work on the row's switch and ⋮, and nothing carries `aria-expanded`, since the page's name isn't a control. → on an expanded row doesn't move the keyboard to its ⋮, as WordPress's does. Dropping a row onto a collapsed parent (moved 40 pixels to the right) puts it last in that submenu; WordPress's wasn't seen.
  - *⋮ is always shown* on a row; WordPress shows it on hover, focus or selection.
  - *Add link.* The popover opens under the "Page link" row, not beside the sidebar. Any click outside it, including on the site, closes it and takes the "Page link" row away; in WordPress a click on the canvas leaves an empty "Page link" row in the menu. It suggests pages only (WordPress also finds posts, categories and tags). A typed address that starts with `javascript:` isn't accepted; WordPress accepts it. The "Select page" placeholder isn't drawn in the menu on the site. Suggested pages are in the order they were made, newest first; WordPress orders by when they were last changed.
  - *Create page in A and Q's advanced menu.* While the saved menu adds new pages (A's Auto add pages on, Q's advanced menu with Add new pages automatically on), a page made with Create page, published or as a draft, is added as any new page is: one link, at the end of the menu (not where the "Page link" row was), already in the saved menu, with nothing to save. So task 4 doesn't depend on whether the page was made on the Pages screen or in Add link. With the setting off, or in the control, the link goes where the "Page link" row was and waits for Save.
  - *A double-click on the menu on the site* opens the sidebar and then does what a second click does (in the control, selects the Page List; in A, the link). For half a second after a click on the menu opens the sidebar, other clicks in the preview are ignored (a click on another item of the menu is logged as ignored), because the sidebar's arrival moves the preview under the pointer. The same goes for the second click of a double-click on a ⋮ item or on a row whose settings replace the list.
  - *New pages go last.* A page made here goes to the end of page order, as the round's site was specified. In WordPress a new page has order 0, so a Page List showed it first.
  - *An emptied Text is put back* when the field is left. WordPress lets a link have no text.
  - *Undo is one step for one action.* WordPress's is finer in places (adding a link is more than one step).
  - *The save panel's ticks.* Unticking Navigation and saving leaves the menu unsaved, as in WordPress. In Q, page order is saved as one thing: saving any moved page saves where all of them are.
  - *"Site updated."* has no "View site" link, since there's nowhere to go.
  - *After removing a link* the keyboard goes to the next row; in WordPress it's lost.
  - *What's done to pages isn't the editor's.* Adding, renaming, publishing and trashing a page apply at once and aren't in the editor's Undo. Where one touches the menu (a trashed page's links go; a published page is added by auto-add) it's made to the saved menu and the unsaved one alike. The saved menu's auto-add setting is the one that decides, since that's the menu the site acts on: switching it off and publishing a page before saving still adds the page.
- **WordPress 7.0's wording.** The block library that WordPress 7.0 ships (9.40.2, from source) words the Page List step differently: a notice in the Page List's settings with the same sentence but "Editing it will enable you…", an "Edit" button beside it, and an "Edit Page List" dialog with "Cancel" and "Edit". The control uses the Gutenberg plugin's current wording, as seen running.
- **A** is a Navigation block whose menu was made as links, with no Page List in it, plus one setting saved on the menu that adds a link for each new page, in the way of the auto-add setting in Gutenberg PR [#84508](https://github.com/WordPress/gutenberg/pull/84508) and the `auto_add_pages` setting that [Quinn](../../quinn/)'s advanced menus have. Here it comes without Quinn's pages menu, toggles or Hide: everything else is the control after detaching, departures and all. The classic Menus screen has a setting of the same name, "Auto add pages: Automatically add new top-level pages to this menu"; the prototype uses the name without the sentence. A page published while the switch is off isn't added later, and a link that was removed isn't put back. **Drafts are added too**, as soon as they're made: a departure from PR #84508 and from Quinn's advanced menus, which add a page when it's published; the round's owner asked for it so that A, like Q's pages menu, shows a new page in the menu (as a draft) from the moment it exists.
- **B** is the same conversion, started from the Pages screen and explained in the prototype's own words. "New" would need WordPress to store which pages have been seen since the menu was converted.
- **F** is a Page List that leaves some pages out, which needs a setting on the block for the pages that are off. Its order is page order, and "Put under" changes a page's parent, and so its address.
- **Q** simulates [Quinn](../../quinn/), a Gutenberg pull request: a menu that is only a Page List lists its pages in the Navigation block's sidebar with a toggle each, and "Switch to advanced menu" turns them into links on a menu that still adds new pages. Following Quinn, switching a parent page off in Q hides its child pages too, though their toggles stay on; in F they move up to the top level, as they did in O'Keeffe's first round. Following Quinn again, nothing warns that moving a page under another changes its address. New pages are always made at the top level here, so an advanced menu adds each one at its end. The words of the Switch to advanced menu dialog and its "Switch" button are the prototype's, written from Quinn's description. As in F, Home can't be put under another page and nothing can be put under Home. Changing a row's level is by drag only (40 pixels or more to the right or left, as above), so it can't be done from the keyboard; Move up and Move down move a row within its level. Quinn is built on the Navigation block's list, so Q's rows are dragged by their names and its advanced menu is the control's list of links. A page published while a switch to an advanced menu is unsaved isn't added as a link, since the saved menu is still a pages menu, which shows it anyway.
- **Drafts.** WordPress's Page List leaves drafts out, in the sidebar's list and on the canvas (seen in WordPress 7.1.5 with Gutenberg 24.4.2: Holiday orders, a draft, isn't under the Page List or in the menu, and the editor asks for pages without a status, which gives published ones only), so the control's doesn't show them either. The editor's canvas does show a link to a draft page (made with Create page and Publish off), as its title followed by "(Draft)", with a wavy underline; its Link to says "Draft" and "This link is to a draft page and will not appear on your site until the page is published." (seen). Q's pages menu, which is Quinn's and wasn't seen running, follows that: its drafts that are switched on are drawn that way in the preview's menu. A's links to drafts and those in Q's advanced menu are drawn the same way. The control's links to drafts aren't drawn in the preview, a departure kept so that the control stays as it was; a draft link's settings say "Draft" and the sentence above in all three. None of this counts for the tasks, which read the menu as visitors see it, without drafts.

## Running it

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and nothing loaded from elsewhere. `app.js` and `style.css` in this folder are round 2's own copy of the O'Keeffe engine, so the first round's prototypes in the folder above are unchanged. Each page sets `window.PROTOTYPE = { arm }` to `control`, `auto-add`, `explained`, `switches` or `menu-switches`. Serve the repository root with any static server and open `okeeffe/round-2/`.

The bakery, its pages and its copy are placeholders.
