# O'Keeffe round 2: pages and the menu, one thing or two?

Six prototypes for one user test about a website's pages and the menu at the top of the site.

**This round's test uses four of them: the control, A, Q and S.** All four change the menu in its own sidebar, as WordPress does. B and F, which change it on the Pages screen, are kept for reference and still work, but aren't in the test. The test has five tasks; the fourth, adding a link to another website, doesn't apply to B and F, which can't hold one.

A new block-theme site's header menu is automatic: it shows every published page, and new pages join it on their own. In WordPress today, the first time someone edits that menu it's converted to a list of links, and from then on it's a separate thing from the pages: new pages don't appear in it, and renaming a page doesn't rename its link.

The test asks two questions:

1. **Do people understand pages and the menu as one thing or two?** After changing the menu, can they say where a page went, and whether the next new page will be in the menu?
2. **Can they change the menu?** Get a new page in, take a page out and put it back, add a link to another website, and reorder it.

It compares two things, crossed: what the menu *is* after you change it (converted to links, or still connected to the pages), and *where* you change it (on the Pages screen, or in the menu's own sidebar, opened by clicking the menu on the site). A fifth prototype, A, asks a narrower question: is it enough to keep new pages arriving in a menu of links, with nothing else about WordPress changed?

Two things follow from the model, and they are what the test is about:

- **Renaming.** While the menu is automatic or connected, a menu item's words are the page's title: rename the page and the menu follows, and there's no separate label. Once a menu has been converted to links, each link has its own label, copied from the page's title when it was converted (or when the link was added). After that, renaming the page doesn't change the link, and changing the link's label doesn't rename the page.
- **New pages.** An automatic or connected menu shows a page as soon as it's published, at its place in page order, which for a new page is the end. A menu that has been converted to links doesn't. Two menus of links are exceptions: Q's advanced menu adds a link for each newly published page until "Add new pages automatically" is turned off, and A's menu does the same until "Auto add pages" is turned off. Neither adds drafts. **A draft joins the menu only when it's published, unless it's added from the menu's own Add link** (Create page with Publish off, in the control, A, Q and S's and S's advanced menus): that link is drawn on the site as WordPress's canvas draws a link to a draft, "Gift cards (Draft)" with a wavy underline, and visitors see it once the page is published. A link added that way is a link like any other: it has its own label, it can be removed, and removing it doesn't bring it back.

In B and F, clicking a link in the preview's menu visits that page. In the control, A, Q and S, clicking the menu selects it, as it does in the editor, so pages are visited from the Pages screen (Edit or View); with the menu selected, a click on one of its links selects that link (or, in the control, the Page List that a page belongs to) and shows its settings. The sidebar is closed by its close button, by Escape, or by clicking anywhere else in the preview, and a reload leaves it as it was.

The control, A, Q and S share one sidebar and one way of saving. The list of links is the same in all four, apart from Hide, Show and "Add new pages automatically", which only Q's and S's advanced menus have, and "Auto add pages", which only A has. A is the control after detaching, plus that switch: the same ⋮ menus, settings, Add link, save panel and log lines for the same actions.

### Saving, in the control, A, Q and S

These four change the menu inside WordPress's editor, so they have the editor's saving, and B and F, which are designs for the Pages screen, don't:

- **A change to the menu is unsaved until Save.** The preview shows it at once, as the editor's canvas does, but the site hasn't changed. **Save**, at the right of the top bar, is pale until there is something to save. It opens the save panel in the sidebar's place: Cancel and Save, "Are you ready to save?", "There is 1 site change waiting to be saved.", and a ticked **Navigation** under **Navigation Menus** (in Q, each moved page under **Pages** too). Saving shows "Site updated."
- **Undo and Redo** are two arrows in the top bar, and Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z and Ctrl+Y (Cmd on a Mac). They go back and forward through every change to the menu, as many steps as there are, including across a save: undoing after saving leaves the undone change waiting to be saved again. A change to the menu shows no message.
- **What's done to pages is not part of that.** Add page, Rename, Publish and Move to trash (in the control, A, Q and S: Add Page, Rename…, the top bar's Publish and Trash…) apply at once in every prototype, with a message and its Undo. Ctrl+Z straight after one of them takes that back; otherwise it's the editor's Undo.
- **The tasks count once the change is saved**, in the notes panel and in the completion code. The code also says what was on screen but not saved.

In B and F every change applies at once, with the message's Undo.

## What's the same in all six

Anything that differs beyond the table above, and the saving described above, is a bug.

- **The site**: Rosa's Bakery, an invented bakery. Its pages, in page order: Home; About, with child pages Our story and Team; Bread and cakes; Catering; Visit us; Thank you for your order. (B and F, which aren't in the test, also have Holiday orders, a draft, from an earlier task.) The header menu starts automatic, so it reads Home, About (Our story, Team), Bread and cakes, Catering, Visit us, Thank you for your order. There's no footer menu.
- **The shell**: a top bar and a bar along the bottom with **Finished**. Nothing in the participant's view names the prototype or switches to another one, and there's no Reset there. In B and F, which are designs for the Pages screen, the top bar has the site's icon, "Pages" and "Rosa's Bakery", the Pages panel is on the left and the site on the right, under the label "Your site". The control, A, Q and S have WordPress's site editor instead, the same in all four (see "The site editor" below): the top bar has the site's icon, "Rosa's Bakery", Undo, Redo and Save, and the pages are on the site editor's Pages screen.
- **The preview** shows the header menu, the current page's title and placeholder content, and "Draft: only you can see this page" on a draft. It updates straight away. The menu doesn't show hidden links or drafts, apart from a link to a draft, which the control, A, Q and S draw as the editor's canvas does, "Gift cards (Draft)" (see "New pages" above). Above it are the label "Your site", the same in every prototype, and the page's address.
- **The Pages panel's basics** (B and F): the pages as a tree, a Draft badge, **Add page** (a title, then Save as draft or Publish), and a ⋮ menu on each row with **Rename**, View on site, Publish (on a draft) and Move to trash (not on Home). Move to trash asks first, with nothing about the menu: "Move "Catering" to trash?", "Visitors won't be able to see this page any more.", Cancel and Move to trash. Clicking a page's title shows it in the preview, whether or not it's in the menu. The control, A, Q and S have WordPress's Pages screen instead (below). Neither counts the pages in the menu.
- **Undo of what's done to pages**: adding, renaming, publishing and trashing a page each show a message at the bottom with Undo; in B and F so does every change to the menu. The message stays for 12 seconds, and longer while the pointer is over it or the keyboard is in it. Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on a Mac) does the same as the button and still works after the message has gone. Undo is one level: it takes back the last change, until the next change is made. The shortcut is left alone while typing in a field or while a dialog is open, and the message is never shown with a dialog open. Undo closes anything that's open first.
- **Reordering** is by drag and by Move up and Move down wherever a prototype lets things be reordered. In B and F a row has a handle, which also takes the up and down arrow keys, and a move shows ""Catering" moved". In the control, A, Q and S a row is dragged by its name, as in the editor. A drag let go of outside its list, or ended with Escape, moves nothing.
- **Dialogs** close with Cancel, Escape or a click outside, and a double-click on the button that opens one doesn't close it again. Escape only ever closes the topmost thing.
- **The tasks, the log, the Finished button and the completion code.**
- **The browser tab's title**, "Rosa's Bakery · Pages". The address still names the prototype (`control.html`, `auto-add.html`, `explained.html`, `switches.html`, `menu-switches.html`); hide it or use a link shortener if that matters for your test.
- **A reload keeps everything.** The site, the log and the counts are kept for the browser tab, so an accidental reload, or a testing tool reopening the link, doesn't start the participant again. A new tab starts fresh. Each prototype keeps its own.

### The site editor, in the control, A, Q and S

These four put the menu inside a simulation of WordPress's site editor, as it was seen running (see "How it maps to WordPress"), the same in all four:

- **Design.** A dark column on the left: a back arrow ("Go to the Dashboard", which does nothing but is logged), **Design**, "Customize the appearance of your website using the block editor.", and the entries **Identity, Styles, Pages, Navigation, Patterns, Templates**, in WordPress's order. The main area is the site, which is edited as before: click the header menu for its sidebar. At the column's foot, "✓ Saved", or, with something unsaved, a blue **Review N changes…** that opens the same save panel as Save.
- **Pages.** The column becomes **Pages**, with a back arrow to Design and the filters All Pages, Published, Scheduled, Drafts, Pending, Private and Trash. The main area becomes WordPress's Pages screen: a list column (**Pages**, a blue **Add Page**, Search, and a row per page with a grey square for its picture, the title, "Homepage" on Home, and its status, "Published" or "Draft", in WordPress's order: by title, A to Z, child pages straight after their parent) and, beside it, a preview of the selected page (the front page until a row is clicked). A row's **Edit**, or a click on the preview, goes back to Design with that page on the site. Its ⋮ (Actions) has WordPress's items in WordPress's order: **Edit, View, Trash…, Duplicate…, Rename…, Order…** and, on a published page other than Home, Set as homepage… and Set as posts page…; Duplicate… and the two Set as… are greyed (not part of the prototype; a click is logged). There's no Publish there, as in WordPress. Home has no Trash… (a departure: WordPress offers it, but the prototype has no site without its front page). **Add Page** asks for a title in WordPress's "Draft new: Page" dialog (Title, placeholder "No title"; Cancel, Create draft): the draft is made, and opens on the site. That's also how any draft is published: Edit, then the top bar's **Publish**.
- **Order…** is WordPress's dialog: "Order", "Determines the order of pages. Pages with the same order value are sorted alphabetically. Negative order values are supported.", a number field ORDER holding the page's number, Cancel and Save. Each page has the number WordPress gave the round's pages (Home 0, About 1, Our story 2, Team 3, Bread and cakes 4, Catering 5, Visit us 6, Thank you for your order 7); page order is these numbers, then the titles, as in WordPress. Save applies it at once, like Rename, with "Order updated." and Undo; it isn't a menu change, so there's nothing to save and the editor's Undo doesn't take it back. Whatever follows page order follows it: the control's Page List, a Page List added with Add block, Q's pages menu, and so the menu on the site and task 3. As in WordPress, Catering 1 ties with About 1 and comes after it, so Catering straight after Home takes two numbers (Catering 1 and About 2), or a number below Home's (which puts it first). What's typed is taken as a whole number (0.5 is 0; nothing is 0). A new page goes last, after the highest number; moving pages in Q's pages menu numbers them afresh, in order.
- **The top bar's button** is WordPress's page editor's: while the page on the site is a draft and nothing else is waiting to be saved, "✓ Saved" and **Publish**, which publishes it at once; otherwise **Save**, which opens the save panel (pale with nothing to save). With a draft and unsaved menu changes it's Save, and the save panel lists only the menu; once that's saved, Publish is back and the page is still a draft. So the page and the menu are never saved together, as in WordPress. **Rename…** is WordPress's Rename dialog (Name; Cancel, Save); **Trash…** asks "Are you sure you want to move "Catering" to the trash?" (Cancel, Trash). Each keeps its message with Undo. The Trash filter lists pages in the trash, without actions.
- **Navigation.** The column becomes **Navigation**: "Navigation Menus are a curated collection of blocks that allow visitors to get around your site.", then the menu's list, the same list as the menu's sidebar (the same rows, toggles, ⋮ menus, Add link, settings, dragging and collapsing), editing the same menu, unsaved until Save or Review changes. It's a second way to change the menu. The main area is the site as a preview; a click on it goes back to Design.
- **Identity, Styles, Patterns, Templates** each open a screen of their own in the column that says it isn't part of this prototype, with a back arrow, and are logged.
- Opening any screen other than Design closes the menu's sidebar. A reload keeps the screen, the Pages screen's filter, search and selected row.

The prototypes are for desktop browsers, from 1024 to 1500 pixels wide.

## Facilitator notes

Add `?notes` or `#notes` to the address (`switches.html?notes`) for a panel on the right that participants shouldn't see. It has:

- the prototype's letter and a line describing it;
- the five tasks. Each is ticked, with the time, when the site reaches the result, and each shows where it stands now. - Control | A | B | F | Q links to switch prototype, keeping the panel open, and **Reset**, which puts the site back and clears the log, the counts and what was kept for the tab.

## The completion code

**Finished**, in the bar at the bottom, first asks "Have you done every task?", with a way back. Then it shows a code to copy into the next question of an unmoderated testing tool, for example `OC-21111-ml140-d1.1-f35-r1.2-o0-n0-x0-u2.1-k3-g40-y30-e0-w0-v2.1-s412`. "Keep editing" goes back, and clicking Finished again gives a new code.

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `OC`, `OA`, `OB`, `OF`, `OQ`, `OS` | The prototype: the control, A, B, F, Q, S. |

## Running a test

Each prototype opens in a desktop browser with no sign-in. Use Reset in the notes panel between participants, or a new tab.

### Who

People who run, or are about to make, a small site: a business, a club, a portfolio. Not developers or theme authors, and not people who already know how WordPress menus work. Five or six per prototype finds the big problems.

### Design

One prototype per participant, since the first one teaches how the menu works and that carries over to the next. Change the order you run them in from day to day.

**Don't say "navigation", "link", "convert", "detach" or "automatic" before they do.** The tasks say what Rosa wants, not where to do it. Task 1 has a prediction to ask *before* the task starts: ask it, then read the task.

**In the control, A, Q and S a change to the menu counts only once it's saved**, as in WordPress. Don't mention Save. Watch whether people save, when (after each change, or once at the end), and what they make of the save panel. If someone finishes without saving, the notes panel shows the tasks as "On screen, not saved" and the code has the dotted task part and a `w` above 0. In a moderated session, once they've said they're finished, ask: "Are those changes on the website now?"

Start with, in the control, A, Q and S: "This is where the owner of a website for a bakery, Rosa's Bakery, makes changes to it. The website is on the right; on the left are the parts of the site she can change." In B and F: "This is the Pages screen of a website for a bakery, Rosa's Bakery. On the right is the website." Then: "Rosa has asked you to make a few changes. Please think aloud as you go. There are no wrong answers; we're testing the design, not you."

### Tasks

The five tasks can each be done in every one of the four arms in the test; how is under each. They follow on: task 3 puts back what task 2 took out, and task 5 comes after that.

1. Ask before they start: **"When you publish a new page, will it show in the menu by itself?"** (Yes, automatically · No, I'll have to add it · Not sure)
   Then: "Rosa is starting to sell gift cards. Make a Gift cards page and publish it." Then: "Rosa wants it in the menu. Make sure it is."
   After: **"Did it appear in the menu by itself?"** (Yes · No, I had to add it · I couldn't get it in · Not sure)
   *How:* in all four, the site editor's **Pages** entry, **Add Page** ("Draft new: Page", Create draft), then the top bar's **Publish** with the new page on the site. The control's Page List shows it by itself; A adds it at the end while Auto add pages is on; Q's pages menu shows it, switched on; S lists it under In your menu. If the control was detached first, or A's switch is off, it's added with **+** below the menu's list (Search or type URL lists it) and Save. It can also be made from the menu's sidebar, in all four: in the control and A (and Q's and S's advanced menus) with **+** and **Create page** in Add link: published at once, with its link (waiting for Save in the control); in Q's pages menu with the **+** below the list, and in S with **+ New page in menu**, each opening the same Create page form: published at once and in the menu, with nothing to save. With Publish off there it's a draft with a link, drawn "Gift cards (Draft)" on the site, which counts once the page is published.
   *Watch for:* whether they find **Pages** in the site editor's navigation (the code's `g` part is the seconds until they first did, and the log says how they got there), whether they find Publish in the top bar after Create draft, whether they make the page from the menu's sidebar instead (the log says where each page was made), and what they predicted and why. A draft never appears in the menu on the site by itself in any arm (in Q it's listed in the pages menu, with its toggle off and unavailable until it's published; in S it's under Other pages, its + unavailable): do people expect it to?
2. "Rosa has stopped doing catering for now. Take Catering out of the menu, but keep the page."
   After: **"Where is the Catering page now? Can a visitor still get to it?"**
   *How:* the control: click the menu on the site (or the **Navigation** entry), then a page under the Page List, or the Page List's settings, gives the **Detach Page List** dialog; Detach, then Catering's ⋮ **Remove Catering**, and Save. (Remove Page List takes every page out, which also counts.) A: Catering's ⋮ Remove, and Save. Q: Catering's toggle off, and Save. S: Catering's **−** in In your menu (it moves to Other pages), and Save.
   *Watch for:* where they look first, the page or the menu on the site; whether they find that the menu can be clicked, or the Navigation entry; in the control, what they make of the Page List and the Detach Page List dialog ("new pages will no longer be added automatically"); whether anyone reaches for **Trash…** (the page then isn't published, and the task isn't done: the `x` part counts pages left in the trash).
3. "Catering is back. Put it back in the menu."
   After: **"Where in the menu did it go? Is that where it was before?"**
   *How:* Q: Catering's toggle on; S: Catering's **+** under Other pages: either way it goes back to its old place, between Bread and cakes and Visit us. The control (detached) and A: **+** below the list, then Catering from Search or type URL (or Add before / Add after on another item): a new link, at the end unless they put it elsewhere, then Save. (Undo after task 2 also puts it back, and counts.)
   *Watch for:* whether they expect it back where it was, and what they make of where it lands; in the control and A, whether they find + and the page in Search or type URL, and whether they move it back; in S, whether they look for Catering under Other pages, and read its + as "put it back".
4. "Rosa's Bakery has an Instagram page at instagram.com/rosasbakery. Add it to the menu so visitors can find it there."
   *How:* the control and A: **+** below the list (or an item's Add before or Add after), type the address into Search or type URL and press Enter or click ↵ (Apply); an address without `https://` is accepted, and the link's label is the address as typed, less any `https://` (renaming it doesn't matter). A link's Link to can also be changed to the address afterwards, which counts too. In the control this works beside the Page List too (the code's `m` part then reads `mp`). Q's and S's pages menus have no way to add a link: **Create menu** first (its dialog's trigger is logged; the code reads `mv`), then the same. Then Save.
   *Watch for:* in Q and S, whether the pages menu stops people or they find Create menu, whether they think it makes a second menu (it doesn't: it turns this one into links), and whether they think new pages still arrive afterwards (they do); in the control, beside the Page List or detach first (`mp` or `ml`), and if they detach, whether they notice that new pages stop arriving.
5. "Rosa wants Visit us to come straight after Home in the menu."
   After: **"Did anything else change order anywhere?"**
   *How:* in all four, drag Visit us by its name in the menu's list, or its ⋮ **Move up** (as many times as it takes: past About, Bread and cakes and, where it's still before it, Catering), then Save. In the control a Page List's pages can't be moved without detaching, and the Pages screen's **Order…** changes page order, which the Page List and Q's and S's pages menus follow (Visit us 0 ties with Home and, as V comes after H, sorts straight after it; Visit us 1 ties with About and sorts after it), so Order… also works there, saved at once; A's links don't follow page order. The 40-pixel rule applies: a drag only puts Visit us under another item if it's moved 40 pixels or more to the right, and Undo puts it back.
   *Watch for:* in the control and A, where Catering went in task 3 changes how far Visit us has to move; in Q and S, Catering is back in its old place, so it's the same as at the start. Drag or Move up, or Order… (the `o` part); in Q and S the move also changes page order, which the Pages screen doesn't show (it lists by title): do they expect it to?

Then click Finished.

### Questions to close with

- **"Rosa is going to add another page next month. When she publishes it, will it appear in the menu by itself?"** (Yes, automatically · No, she'll have to add it · Not sure). Scored against whether the saved menu at the end still adds new pages, which the code's `m` part says: `ma`, and `mp` while the control's Page List is still there: yes; `ml` and `mr`: no; `mv`, with or without its `p`: yes; `mx`, with or without its `p`: no (Add new pages automatically or Auto add pages is off in the saved menu).
- "What's the difference between a page and the menu?"

### What to watch for

- **The prediction before task 1 and the closing prediction.** This is the main measure of the first question. Compare what people say with what the prototype does, per prototype. A right answer with a reason ("because I took one out, it stopped adding them") is worth more than a right guess. The closing question comes after the person's own changes, so it asks about the menu as they've left it, and nothing on screen answers it.
- **Tasks 2 and 3 together.** Where Catering comes back: in Q and S its old place, by the toggle or the +; in the detached control and in A a new link at the end, or wherever they add it. Whether the answer to "Is that where it was before?" matches.
- **Time to the first change to the menu** (the `f` part), and how long before the menu on the site, or the Navigation entry, is clicked at all (`k` and `y`).
- **Dialogs confirmed without reading.** Ask what it said. The `d` part gives shown and confirmed.
- **Saving, in the control, A, Q and S.** Whether people notice Save at all, whether the save panel stops them, and whether they leave with changes unsaved (the `w` and `v` parts, and the dotted task digits).
- **Task 4 in Q and S, and the control's route to it.** Whether the pages menu stops people, or they find Create menu; in the control, beside the Page List or detach first (the `m` part).
- **Words.** Note what people call the menu ("the tabs", "the top bar", "the links") and whether "in your menu", "Navigation", "Page List" and "Detach" mean anything to them.

### What would favour each

- **Connected (F, Q and S)**: the predictions are right more often than in the control and B, Catering comes back where people expect in task 3, and nobody is surprised that reordering the menu reorders the pages.
- **Converting (the control and B)**: people want the menu and the pages to be separate, for example a menu label that differs from the page's title, or a menu order that differs from page order, and they understand that new pages need adding.
- **B over the control**: the confirm and the markers lead to right predictions where the control's Detach Page List dialog doesn't.
- **A over the control**: A differs from the control in two ways, not one: there is no Page List and no Detach step, and the switch keeps new pages arriving. So a gap on task 2 comes from the missing step, and only task 1 and the predictions show what the switch does. In A's favour: the predictions are right and the new pages are where people expect, while the menu is still changed the way it is in WordPress. If people in A do as well on those tasks as in F and Q, keeping new pages arriving may be enough by itself.
- **A not being enough**: people leave Auto add pages alone and still can't say whether a new page will be in the menu; they turn it off without knowing what it does; or they expect Catering back in its old place in task 3, which the connected prototypes give them.
- **S over Q**: the same model, drawn as two lists rather than toggles. In S's favour: task 2 is started sooner, people look under Other pages for Catering in task 3 without being told, and they can say before task 1 that a new page will be "in your menu" because the heading says where pages go; the predictions are right more often than in Q. Against: people read − as deleting the page, or don't see that Other pages are still on the site, which the footnote is there to prevent.
- **Create menu (Q and S)**: in their favour, people find Create menu for task 4 without help, and say from its dialog that new pages will still arrive. Against: they read it as making a second menu, or think the Pages list in the sidebar isn't the site's menu until they press it.
- **Q over S**: a toggle beside each page is read faster than a row moving from one list to another, and in S people lose track of Catering after −, or look for it in the menu's ⋮.
- **The Pages screen (B and F) over the sidebar (the control, A, Q and S)**: tasks 2, 3 and 5 are started sooner and finished more often. Saving is part of this comparison, not a flaw in it: B and F are designs in which a change applies at once, and the sidebar arms are WordPress's editor, where it waits for Save. A participant who does everything in a sidebar arm and never saves has not changed the site, and the code says so.
- **The sidebar over the Pages screen**: people go to the menu on the site first and find what they need there, or say that changing the menu from a list of pages is odd.

## How it maps to WordPress

- **The automatic menu** is a Navigation block whose menu holds one Page List block, which is what a new block theme's header falls back to. Page List shows every published page in page order (`menu_order`), child pages as submenus.
- **Converting to links** is the Page List's own Detach step, which replaces it with a Page Link block for each page. Each link stores its own label.
- **What the control was checked against.** A running editor: WordPress 7.1.4 with the Gutenberg plugin 24.4.1 (the site editor's navigation, Pages and Navigation screens later, in 7.1.5 with 24.4.2), Twenty Twenty-Five and this site's pages, in WordPress Playground, driven step by step with the header's Navigation block selected. Collapsing rows and Add block were checked later, in WordPress 7.1.5 with Gutenberg 24.4.2, the same way. Everything in this list was seen there unless it says "from source", which means read in `@wordpress/block-library` 12.1.0 and `@wordpress/block-editor` 20.0.0 and not seen running.
- **The Page List step, as seen.** The first click on the menu on the canvas selects the Navigation block; a second click on a page selects the Page List, and further clicks do nothing more: a click on the canvas never opens the Detach dialog. In the sidebar's list, the Page List row has the pages under it, and clicking one of those opens the dialog at once. Clicking the Page List row replaces the list with the Page List's settings, with a back arrow, a Parent field and an outlined "Detach Page List" button. The dialog: title "Detach Page List", a close button, "This Navigation Menu displays your website's pages. Detaching will enable you to add, delete, or reorder pages. However, new pages will no longer be added automatically.", "Cancel" and "Detach"; the close button is labelled "Cancel" too. It takes the keyboard itself, Enter confirms it and Escape cancels it (seen when the control was checked again in the same versions); the control does the same, and so does Q's and S's Create menu dialog (Quinn's Switch to advanced menu), which is also a ConfirmDialog in Quinn. The Page List row's ⋮, in order: Move up, Move down, Add submenu link (all three unavailable while it's alone), Duplicate, Add before, Add after, Remove Page List. Remove Page List asks nothing and leaves "This Navigation Menu is empty." Starting to drag a page under the Page List opens the dialog at once, before it's let go of, wherever it's then dropped (seen); so does the control. A double-click on a page in the menu on the canvas, with nothing selected, selects the Page List, as two clicks do (seen).
- **Links, as seen.** After detaching, each row is a name and a ⋮: Move up, Move down, Add submenu link, Duplicate, Add before, Add after, Remove followed by the name. Rows have no handles and drag whole. A row with rows under it (the Page List, a page with child pages under it, a link with a submenu) has a chevron before its name, pointing down: everything starts expanded. Clicking the chevron hides or shows the rows under it without selecting anything, ← and → on the row do the same, and the row's name says which (`aria-expanded`; the chevron is hidden from screen readers). The list forgets it: after selecting a row and going back, or a reload, everything is expanded again. A collapsed row dragged elsewhere takes its rows along and stays collapsed. Clicking a row replaces the list with the link's settings: Text, Link to (a button: the page's title, address and "Page" and "Published"; for a typed address, the address and "External link"), and more below. + (labelled "Add page"; and Add before, Add after, Add submenu link) puts a "Page link" row in the list and opens a popover: "Search or type URL", the site's pages newest first, then "Create page" and "Add block". A click on another row or on empty space in the sidebar, or the top bar's Undo, closes it and takes the "Page link" row away; Undo then undoes nothing else. A click on the canvas closes it but leaves an empty "Page link" row behind. Create page puts the keyboard on Back first. Typing searches; a typed address and Enter, or the ↵ (Apply) button inside the field, makes a link labelled with the address less any `http://` or `https://` (`https://www.instagram.com/rosasbakery/` gives "www.instagram.com/rosasbakery/"); Apply is unavailable until something is typed; Escape takes the "Page link" row away. **Add block**, after Create page, shows Back (which takes the keyboard), "Blocks" and six blocks: Page link (back to Search or type URL), Custom Link (the same, the row reading "Custom Link", without Create page; a typed address makes a link), Buttons (a Buttons block with an empty Button, drawn as a dark "Add text…" pill), Page List (a second Page List), Search (a search field and button), Social Icons (empty, drawn as a small outlined +), then Browse all. Create page asks for a Title (placeholder "No title") and has a Publish switch, on, with "Turn off to save as a draft. Drafts won't appear on your site until published.", then Cancel and Create page; the page is made at once, before any Save. A picked page's link is labelled with the page's title. Duplicate selects the copy.
- **Saving and undo, as seen.** No change to the menu shows a message. Undo and Redo are in the top bar (Ctrl+Z; Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z), many levels, and still work after a save, leaving unsaved changes. Save reads "Save" and is pale until there's something to save. It opens the save panel where the sidebar was: Cancel and Save, "Are you ready to save?", "There is 1 site change waiting to be saved.", and under "Navigation Menus" a ticked "Navigation". After saving: "Site updated." Typing in Text changes the menu as it's typed, and one Undo takes all of it back. That a moved page is listed under "Pages" in Q is worked out from the editor's name for pages, not seen: stock WordPress can't move pages from the menu, and Quinn's build wasn't run.
- **Where the control still departs from WordPress, and why.**
  - *The route in.* The site editor's navigation, Pages screen and Navigation screen follow WordPress's (seen in 7.1.5 with Gutenberg 24.4.2), but on Design the main area is the site itself, editable, with the top bar's Undo, Redo and Save, and a click on the header menu opens its sidebar. In WordPress, Design's main area is a preview of the front page with no top bar; a click on it opens the editor on that page, where a click on the header selected the locked Header template part (with "Edit original"), not the menu, and the header's menu wasn't drawn at all. So in WordPress the menu is changed on the Navigation screen or by editing the Header template part; clicking the menu on the site is this round's given. There is no template part, block toolbar, breadcrumb, block inserter or Styles, so the toolbar's own "Detach" button is missing; the sidebar's button and the dialog are there.
  - *The Pages screen.* In WordPress, Edit opens the page in the editor, where the top bar's first button goes back to the Pages screen, and View opens the published page in a new tab; here both go to Design with the page on the site. The rows' pictures are grey squares; there are no filter, layout or view-options buttons; Search matches titles only; Duplicate…, Set as homepage… and Set as posts page… are greyed; Trash lists trashed pages without Restore or Delete; Home has no Trash… (WordPress offers it). An emptied Order… field is saved as 0: not confirmed in WordPress, where the emptied field showed 0 again before Save. WordPress gives a new page the number 0; here it gets the next number, so it goes last, as the round's site was specified. The status filters are filters, without WordPress's "Status includes" filter bar beyond a chip. The messages are the prototype's own, with Undo (WordPress's: '"Gift cards" successfully created.', "Page published."). On the Pages screen the message sits over the preview, clear of the list, so it doesn't cover its ⋮ buttons.
  - *The Navigation screen* shows the same list as the menu's sidebar. WordPress's leaves the Page List row out (its pages are listed at the top level), its ⋮ has Move up, Move down, Go to <page> and Remove, a click on a link's name does nothing, and the heading's ⋮ has Rename, Edit, Duplicate and Delete; here the rows, ⋮ menus and settings are the sidebar's, the heading has no ⋮, and in Q the toggles are there too (Quinn's own screen wasn't seen).
  - *Identity, Styles, Patterns and Templates* each open a screen that says it isn't part of the prototype; in WordPress, Identity and Styles keep the Design column.
  - *Opening another screen closes the menu's sidebar*, and the Pages screen keeps the site's preview beside the list rather than WordPress's iframe of the page.
  - *The sidebar is the list and the settings only.* WordPress's has tabs (list, settings, styles), a menu chooser beside the "Navigation" heading, and, in a link's settings, Open in new tab, Edit, View, Description and Rel attribute; in the Page List's, Parent and Advanced. None of these are here. Parent would change which pages the Page List shows, which the tasks don't allow for.
  - *Dragging is kinder.* In WordPress a row dropped on another with the pointer over its name becomes that row's child, with no sideways movement at all: dragging Catering straight up onto Home, held by its name, put it in Home's submenu. Here a row changes level only after 40 pixels of sideways movement, the same in the control, A, Q and S. Copying WordPress would have made task 3 fail for most people who drag, for a reason that isn't what the test is about.
  - *Two levels.* Links go two levels deep, so Add submenu link is unavailable on a link that's already in a submenu; in WordPress it isn't.
  - *Link to.* In WordPress it's a button; what clicking it shows wasn't seen. Here it opens a field with the link's address (a page link's is its path, such as `/catering`) and ↵ (Apply) inside it: Enter or Apply changes where the link goes, if what's typed is an address, and keeps its text; Escape leaves it as it was. A page link changed this way becomes a link to that address. It's logged, and a change to Rosa's Instagram counts for task 4.
  - *Add block.* There is no Browse all (it opens the editor's full inserter, which isn't here). In a submenu it offers Page link and Custom Link only; what WordPress offers there wasn't seen. The other blocks have no settings: selecting one shows its description, and selecting the Button inside Buttons selects the Buttons. They can be moved, duplicated and removed, can't have a submenu or go in one, and count for the tasks only by being in the menu (Search between Home and Catering means Catering isn't straight after Home). In A and Q's advanced menu a Page List added this way shows every published page and can be detached, with WordPress's Detach Page List dialog (in Q and S too, not Create menu).
  - *Collapsing rows.* Q's pages menu has chevrons too (on a page with child pages), on the assumption that Quinn's list is the editor's list view; Quinn's build wasn't run. There ← and → work on the row's switch and ⋮, and nothing carries `aria-expanded`, since the page's name isn't a control. → on an expanded row doesn't move the keyboard to its ⋮, as WordPress's does. Dropping a row onto a collapsed parent (moved 40 pixels to the right) puts it last in that submenu; WordPress's wasn't seen.
  - *⋮ is always shown* on a row; WordPress shows it on hover, focus or selection.
  - *Add link.* The popover opens under the "Page link" row, not beside the sidebar. Any click outside it, including on the site, closes it and takes the "Page link" row away; in WordPress a click on the canvas leaves an empty "Page link" row in the menu. It suggests pages only (WordPress also finds posts, categories and tags). A typed address that starts with `javascript:` isn't accepted; WordPress accepts it. The "Select page" placeholder isn't drawn in the menu on the site. Suggested pages are in the order they were made, newest first; WordPress orders by when they were last changed.
  - *Create page in A and Q's advanced menu.* While the saved menu adds new pages (A's Auto add pages on, Q's advanced menu with Add new pages automatically on), a page published with Create page is added as any published page is: one link, at the end of the menu (not where the "Page link" row was), already in the saved menu, with nothing to save. So task 1 doesn't depend on whether the page was made on the Pages screen or in Add link. With the setting off, or in the control, or with Publish off (a draft, which the setting never adds), the link goes where the "Page link" row was and waits for Save.
  - *A double-click on the menu on the site* opens the sidebar and then does what a second click does (in the control, selects the Page List; in A, the link). For half a second after a click on the menu opens the sidebar, other clicks in the preview are ignored (a click on another item of the menu is logged as ignored), because the sidebar's arrival moves the preview under the pointer. The same goes for the second click of a double-click on a ⋮ item or on a row whose settings replace the list.
  - *New pages go last.* A page made here goes to the end of page order, as the round's site was specified. In WordPress a new page has order 0, so a Page List showed it first.
  - *An emptied Text is put back* when the field is left. WordPress lets a link have no text.
  - *Undo is one step for one action.* WordPress's is finer in places (adding a link is more than one step).
  - *The save panel's ticks.* Unticking Navigation and saving leaves the menu unsaved, as in WordPress. In Q, page order is saved as one thing: saving any moved page saves where all of them are.
  - *"Site updated."* has no "View site" link, since there's nowhere to go.
  - *After removing a link* the keyboard goes to the next row; in WordPress it's lost.
  - *What's done to pages isn't the editor's.* Adding, renaming, publishing and trashing a page apply at once and aren't in the editor's Undo. Where one touches the menu (a trashed page's links go; a published page is added by auto-add) it's made to the saved menu and the unsaved one alike. The saved menu's auto-add setting is the one that decides, since that's the menu the site acts on: switching it off and publishing a page before saving still adds the page.
- **WordPress 7.0's wording.** The block library that WordPress 7.0 ships (9.40.2, from source) words the Page List step differently: a notice in the Page List's settings with the same sentence but "Editing it will enable you…", an "Edit" button beside it, and an "Edit Page List" dialog with "Cancel" and "Edit". The control uses the Gutenberg plugin's current wording, as seen running.
- **A** is a Navigation block whose menu was made as links, with no Page List in it, plus one setting saved on the menu that adds a link for each new page, in the way of the auto-add setting in Gutenberg PR [#84508](https://github.com/WordPress/gutenberg/pull/84508) and the `auto_add_pages` setting that [Quinn](../../quinn/)'s advanced menus have. Here it comes without Quinn's pages menu, toggles or Hide: everything else is the control after detaching, departures and all. The classic Menus screen has a setting of the same name, "Auto add pages: Automatically add new top-level pages to this menu"; the prototype uses the name without the sentence. A page published while the switch is off isn't added later, and a link that was removed isn't put back.
- **B** is the same conversion, started from the Pages screen and explained in the prototype's own words. "New" would need WordPress to store which pages have been seen since the menu was converted.
- **F** is a Page List that leaves some pages out, which needs a setting on the block for the pages that are off. Its order is page order, and "Put under" changes a page's parent, and so its address.
- **Q** simulates [Quinn](../../quinn/), a Gutenberg pull request: a menu that is only a Page List lists its pages in the Navigation block's sidebar with a toggle each, and "Switch to advanced menu" turns them into links on a menu that still adds new pages. Here that button is renamed **Create menu**, with a dialog to match, and the sidebar's heading reads Pages until it's used; both are this round's changes, not Quinn's. Following Quinn, switching a parent page off in Q hides its child pages too, though their toggles stay on; in F they move up to the top level, as they did in O'Keeffe's first round. Following Quinn again, nothing warns that moving a page under another changes its address. New pages are always made at the top level here, so an advanced menu adds each one at its end. The words of the Create menu dialog are the prototype's: Quinn's dialog is titled "Switch to advanced menu", and its words here were written from Quinn's description. As in F, Home can't be put under another page and nothing can be put under Home. Changing a row's level is by drag only (40 pixels or more to the right or left, as above), so it can't be done from the keyboard; Move up and Move down move a row within its level. Quinn is built on the Navigation block's list, so Q's rows are dragged by their names and its advanced menu is the control's list of links. A page published while a switch to an advanced menu is unsaved isn't added as a link, since the saved menu is still a pages menu, which shows it anyway.
- **S** is round 1's D (the [menu sections](../sections.html) prototype, which had In your menu and Other pages on a page of its own) brought into the editor's sidebar, on Q's model. It is not a core or Gutenberg design: nothing in WordPress or in Quinn draws the pages menu this way. Everything outside the pages menu, including Create menu and the advanced menu after it, is Q's, which was checked by a scripted comparison of the two on the same steps. Its − is Q's toggle switched off and its + Q's toggle switched on; drafts are listed as in Q, with + unavailable where Q's toggle is.
- **Drafts.** WordPress's Page List leaves drafts out, in the sidebar's list and on the canvas (seen in WordPress 7.1.5 with Gutenberg 24.4.2: Holiday orders, a draft, isn't under the Page List or in the menu, and the editor asks for pages without a status, which gives published ones only), so the control's doesn't show them either. The editor's canvas does show a link to a draft page (made with Create page and Publish off), as its title followed by "(Draft)", with a wavy underline; its Link to says "Draft" and "This link is to a draft page and will not appear on your site until the page is published." (seen). The prototype does the same in the control, A, Q and S's and S's advanced menus: a link to a draft (which only Create page with Publish off makes; Auto add pages and Add new pages automatically add a page only when it's published) is drawn that way on the site, and its settings say "Draft" and that sentence. Q's pages menu, which is Quinn's and wasn't seen running, lists drafts with a Draft badge and a toggle that's off and unavailable until the page is published, and never shows them in the menu on the site. None of this counts for the tasks, which read the menu as visitors see it, without drafts.

## Running it

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and nothing loaded from elsewhere. `app.js` and `style.css` in this folder are round 2's own copy of the O'Keeffe engine, so the first round's prototypes in the folder above are unchanged. Each page sets `window.PROTOTYPE = { arm }` to `control`, `auto-add`, `explained`, `switches`, `menu-switches` or `menu-sections`. Serve the repository root with any static server and open `okeeffe/round-2/`.
