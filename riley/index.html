This page or every page?

A site's header and footer are shared by every page, but nothing in the editor says so, so people can't tell whether a change affects the page they're on or the whole site. These five prototypes are the same small site in the same simple editor, and differ only in how the header and footer are treated, so a test can tell whether marking shared parts before someone edits them helps, which way of marking works better, and whether saying what happened afterwards is enough.

  • 0 · ControlAs the WordPress editor is todayThe header and footer are edited like the rest of the page. A purple outline, on hover and while editing, is the only sign that they are different. The baseline for the others.
  • A · Shown on hoverA label and a button under the pointer, one click to unlockPointing at the header or footer outlines it and shows "Shown on every page" with an Edit button. One click unlocks it, and it locks again when you work anywhere else.
  • C · Always labelledSite header, Site footer and the page's nameNo lock and no question. The header, the footer and the page's own content each carry a label, and the top bar lists the whole site's parts beside its pages.
  • D · Told after the changeThe control, then a message with UndoLooks like the control. Each change to the header or footer is followed by "Updated on all 3 pages" and an Undo button. It asks nothing and blocks nothing.
  • E · Summary at saveThe control, then a list at SaveLooks like the control. Save lists what changed on every page and what changed only on one page, before saving.

Add ?notes (or #notes) to any of them for the facilitator panel, or open the control, A, C, D or E with it: it lists the tasks and logs every edit, which part it was in, and whether the person looked at another page afterwards. Finished, in the bar at the bottom, gives a code to paste into a testing tool. Reload a page to start again. The test script is in the README. The bike repair shop and its pages are placeholders.
