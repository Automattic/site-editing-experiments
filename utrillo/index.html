Selecting the section around what you clicked

People click what they can see, a paragraph or a picture, and the editor selects that block. Then they have to find how to select the section around it, the Group, Columns, Column or Cover, to move it, change its background or delete it. These three prototypes are the same small page in the same simple editor, with every one of the editor's own ways to a parent block, and differ in one thing each, so a test can tell whether offering the section where people are pointing helps them select it more often than a button they have to find after selecting what's inside.

  • ControlAs the WordPress editor is todayA click selects the innermost block. To get to its section there's the toolbar's Select parent block button, the same in the ⋮ menu, the breadcrumb under the canvas, Document Overview, or a click on the section's own padding. The baseline for the others.
  • A · Hover tabA name tab on each container under the pointerPointing at the page outlines the containers under the pointer, each with a small tab with its name ("Group", "Columns", "Column", "Cover"). Clicking a tab selects that container.
  • D · LayersA Layers button on each sectionEach section has a Layers button at its top-right corner, on hover and while something in it is selected. It lists the section's blocks; clicking one selects it.

Add ?notes (or #notes) to any of them for the facilitator panel, or open the control, A or D with it: it lists the tasks, counts how each section was selected, and logs every selection and change. Finished, in the bar at the bottom, gives a code to paste into a testing tool. Reload a page to start again. The yoga studio and its page are placeholders.
