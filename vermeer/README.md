# Vermeer: from looking at your site to changing it

Prototypes for testing how people get from looking at their site to changing it, and whether they find Save once they have.

The [Create not Learn](https://github.com/Automattic/create-not-learn-editor) prototype opens a page "on view": the site as visitors see it, with links that work. To change it, you press **Edit** in the toolbar above it. Someone who has just made their first site may not know to look there: they may click the heading they want to change, see nothing happen, and click again somewhere else.

The idea being tested: new users find their way into editing faster, and with fewer wrong turns, if the site itself responds to their first click or to the pointer, than if they have to find an Edit button. One task also asks people to make their change visible to visitors, to see how easily they find Save.

## Changes since the first version

- **The editor around the site is Create not Learn's**, drawn after its trunk (commit 54b6612): WordPress's admin bar, the dark strip of icons, the sidebar with Home's places to start and the Pages screen, and the canvas toolbar with the page finder, the View menu and Save with Review changes. It's the same in all five. Before, it was a plain frame with a sidebar holding only the tip.
- **A Save task.** Task 2 asks people to make their new heading visible, without saying "save". The code says when and how the first save was made and what was left unsaved (`v` and `w`), and the task digits say what was on screen as well as what was saved. Clicks on the admin bar, the strip and the sidebar before the first time in editing are counted (`g`).

What's the same in all five:

- **The site**: an invented independent bookshop with a café corner, Cobble Lane Books, with three pages, Home, Events and Contact. Every page has the same header (the shop's name, which goes to Home, a menu of the three pages, and a "Join the book club" button that opens a small sign-up panel) and the same footer (address, phone number, "Open every day exept Mondays" with its spelling mistake, a "How to find us" link, a newsletter sign-up and a copyright line). Home has a big heading, an introduction with a link to Events, a "See what's on" button that goes to Events, a picture, three book picks and a café section. Events lists three events, each with a "Book a place" button that changes to "Place booked ✓". Contact has the opening hours, a contact form, and a "Get directions" link to a map lower down. Forms answer on the page ("Thanks, you're on the list.") and nothing leaves the prototype.
- **The editor around it, as Create not Learn draws it.** Across the top, WordPress's admin bar: the WordPress logo, "Cobble Lane Books", updates "1", search, comments "0", "+ New" and "Howdy, admin". Down the left, the dark strip of icons: Home; Pages and Posts; Site overview, Menus, Name & logo and Media; Colors and Fonts; and at the foot Advanced, Expand sidebar and the workspace switcher. Beside it, the sidebar. On Home it shows the site's name and address and Create not Learn's places to start: Your homepage, Pages, Name & logo, Design, and Header & footer, each with its line, then the tip, "Tip: follow links to look around your site, and press Edit to change the page you're on."
- **The sidebar's screens.** Pages is Create not Learn's Pages screen: "Every page on your site. Pick one to see it, and switch it on to show it in your menu.", the three pages (Homepage, Events, Contact) with their menu switches and ⋮, the + under them, Add page, and System pages and Manage all menus. Clicking a page there shows it in the site, as in Create not Learn. Your homepage opens the Pages screen with the homepage shown. The strip's icons and the other places open their screens with Create not Learn's headings and lines (Posts, Site overview, Menus, Name & logo, Media, Design, Colors, Fonts, Header & footer), with their controls drawn. The back arrow goes back to Home. Everything else in the admin bar, the strip and the sidebar is drawn but does nothing: a click on it is logged as "Tried …".
- **Looking at the site ("browse")**: above the site, Back and Forward for the pages visited, the page's name in a bar with a search icon and a "published" tick (click it for Create not Learn's page finder: "Go to page…", the main pages, Add a page and Manage pages; on the Pages screen the homepage reads "Homepage · Home", as there), ··· on the homepage (Page options, with Manage homepage, which opens the Pages screen), the View menu (Desktop, Tablet, Mobile, Open in a new tab, Open in advanced editor; only Desktop does anything), and the blue **Edit** button with its pencil ("Change this page").
- **Changing it ("edit")**: Edit opens the page you're on to change. The strip and the sidebar go, the admin bar stays, and the site gets wider. The toolbar has Undo, Redo and Document Overview, the page's name (still the page finder), ···, View, Zoom out, **Exit** and **Save**; the zoom controls (+ and −) sit at the bottom right. Document Overview, Zoom out and the zoom controls are drawn but do nothing. Clicking any words (headings, paragraphs, the menu, buttons, links, form labels) puts the caret there, and the section around them is outlined, with its name in a small blue tab ("Hero", "Footer"), as in Create not Learn. Links and buttons don't work while editing, boxes can't be typed in, and pictures can't be changed (a click outlines them). Enter finishes; Escape puts the words back as they were when you clicked in. Escape doesn't leave editing; only Exit does.
- **Save**, as Create not Learn's: always there while editing, greyed while there's nothing to save (still focusable, with its tooltip; a click then does nothing, but is logged), and it pulses when a new change joins the list. Its tooltip says how many changes are waiting ("1 unsaved change"), or "All changes saved", and the shortcut. A click opens **Review changes**: "Visitors see these once you save them. Discard any you don't want to keep.", with the changes to the header or footer under "Site-wide changes" and the changes to a page under "Page changes", each with Discard, then Discard all (which asks first, then says "Changes discarded."), Cancel and "Save 1 change". Focus starts on its ×, as in WordPress's dialogs, so Enter doesn't save at once. Ctrl+S (⌘S on a Mac) saves straight away while editing, also from Review changes or the Exit dialog (where it does what their Save does); while browsing it does nothing, and the browser's own "Save page as" never opens. The site, when you look at it, shows only saved changes.
- **Exit** goes back to looking at the same page. If there are unsaved changes it asks first: "Save your changes?", "Your site only shows saved changes, so unsaved ones won't appear there.", with Save, Discard and Keep editing.
- **The Finished button**, in a bar along the bottom, which asks "Have you done every task?" before it shows the code.
- **The browser tab's title**, "Cobble Lane Books · Your website", so it doesn't name the prototype. The address still does (`control.html`, `click-to-edit.html`, `ask-on-click.html`, `hover-notice.html`, `choose-on-click.html`).

Unlike earlier experiments here, **a reload doesn't start again**. Everything is kept in the browser (the saved site, unsaved changes, editing or not, the page and the back and forward history, the sidebar's screen, what's typed in the site's boxes, the log, the counts and the time since first opening), separately for each prototype, so someone who reloads by accident carries on where they were. **Reset** in the notes panel starts again.

## Decisions

- **Every arm keeps the Edit button.** The question is whether adding a way in through the site helps, not whether it can replace the button. Keeping it means each arm is the control plus one thing, everyone has the same keyboard route, and the log shows which way each person actually went in.
- **Create not Learn's chrome, drawn, not built.** People meet the Edit button among everything else Create not Learn shows, so the test shows it among the same things. The sizes, words, icons and order come from Create not Learn's code; only the canvas works fully. The admin bar, the strip and the sidebar answer a click (screens open, Pages shows a page) or log it as tried, so a wrong turn there is visible in the log and in `g`.
- **Exit asks about unsaved changes, and that gives a second way to save.** Create not Learn's Exit throws unsaved changes away without asking ("Stop editing without saving"); its "Save your changes?" dialog only appears when the page is left another way. Here, two of the six tasks are about looking at the site, so people will leave editing in the middle of the test; losing their earlier changes there would swamp everything else the test measures. So Exit asks, in every arm. That means someone who never finds Save can still save from the dialog. The code keeps the two apart: the `v` part says whether the first save was the toolbar's Save (`t`), the keyboard (`k`) or the Exit dialog (`d`), and how many saves were made through the dialog. Read task 2 together with it: a heading saved only through the dialog says Save itself wasn't found.
- **Clicks go straight to the words.** Create not Learn's editing selects the section first and the words on a later click. That's tested in [Utrillo](../utrillo/). Here a click puts the caret in the words straight away, in every arm, so the only difference between arms is how people get into editing, not what happens once they're in. The look is Create not Learn's (the section outlined, with its name in a tab), but nothing is added inside the site: there's no section toolbar (Add, Edit design, the move arrows, Duplicate, Delete) and no toolbar on the words. That's the remaining difference from Create not Learn.
- **Touch.** On a touch screen there's no hover, so the first touch on the site stands in for it in B: it shows the notice and does nothing else. After that, taps work as usual. The prototypes are for desktop tests; the rule is there so a tablet doesn't break them.
- **B's notice sits at the top right, with its Edit under the real one.** So that taking the notice's way in also teaches where the toolbar's Edit is; the copy is measured against the real button whenever the notice shows or the window changes size, so the two stay lined up. The cost: at the top of the page it lies over the right of the site's header. At 1440 wide it covers the whole menu (Home, Events, Contact) and "Join the book club"; at 1024 wide it covers those and the end of the shop's name ("…Books"). Since it stays while the pointer is anywhere over the site (itself included), the menu can't be clicked while it shows: people have to close it with the ×, go into editing, or scroll the header out from under it. That bears on task 5 (the menu) and task 3 (Contact by the menu). The notice's Edit and × sit right over "Join the book club", so a click meant for that button can press the notice's Edit (accepted, and a first entry by `a`) or its × (dismissed); a click meant for the menu lands on the notice's words and does nothing. On a touch screen it stays until the × or Edit. Since it shows on the first movement, everyone in B sees it within moments of starting; the question for B is whether they take it, not whether they find it.
- **One tab per prototype.** Everything is kept in the browser per prototype, so two tabs of the same one would overwrite each other. The tab that saved last is the one that counts: when another tab of the same prototype saves (or is Reset), the first stops saving and says "This website is open in another tab", with "Carry on here", which reloads it from what the other tab saved. A second tab logs "Opened again in a new tab or window". So don't open the same prototype in a second tab to watch the notes panel: the participant's tab would stop saving. To keep people in one tab, the site's links can't be opened in a new one: a middle click on a link does nothing, Ctrl or Cmd with a click follows it in the same tab, and links have no right-click menu and can't be dragged.
- **Where Create not Learn had to be guessed or left out.**
  - In Create not Learn, Your homepage also opens the homepage for editing. Here it only shows the homepage and opens the Pages screen, so the sidebar isn't another way into editing that the arms don't control.
  - The Pages screen's switches, ⋮ menus, + and Add page; the page finder's Add a page; and the ⋮ menu beside Add page are drawn but do nothing. So does Manage pages while editing, as the sidebar is hidden then.
  - The tip leaves out Create not Learn's second sentence ("Zoom out while editing to add and arrange its sections."), since zooming out does nothing here.
  - The site's name in the sidebar has no badge for unsaved changes. Create not Learn shows one, but there are never unsaved changes while the sidebar shows here, as Exit asks first.
  - Create not Learn shows "Saving…" while it saves; here saving is instant, and Save says "Saving…" for a moment so it looks the same.
  - Ctrl+S saves straight away. Create not Learn's Save button names the shortcut; what the keyboard does there wasn't checked.
  - Exit's description, read by screen readers, is "Stop editing"; Create not Learn's is "Stop editing without saving", which isn't true here as Exit asks.
  - The icons are drawn after `@wordpress/icons` and the admin bar's dashicons, by hand.
  - The Design, Colors, Fonts, Media, Name & logo, Menus, Site overview and Posts screens have Create not Learn's headings and lines, with simple stand-ins for their contents.
- **The keyboard way to Edit goes through all of the chrome.** From the top of the page, Edit is the 31st Tab: through the admin bar, the strip of icons, the sidebar and the toolbar, in the same order as Create not Learn's (the count itself wasn't checked against Create not Learn). B's notice and the arms' boxes don't change that.
- **Smaller things.** A double click on a link or button follows or presses it once, in every arm. Every bit of text is one line: Enter finishes it, in paragraphs too. B doesn't show while the page finder, a menu or a dialog is open.

## Facilitator notes

Add `?notes` or `#notes` to the address (`hover-notice.html?notes`) for a panel on the right that participants shouldn't see. It has:

- the six tasks, ticked with the time when the site reaches the result, and unticked if it's undone. Tasks 2, 4 and 6 tick from the **saved** site; when a change is on screen but not saved they say "On screen, not saved" in amber. Task 1 ticks from what's on screen. Task 5 shows how many of the three menu links have been followed (2/3), and task 3 how Contact was reached. The tick boxes are yours to use as you like; they don't change the code.
- counts: seconds to the first time in editing and by which way; clicks on the site that did nothing, before then and in all; clicks on the admin bar, the strip and the sidebar, before then and in all; things tried that are only drawn; times into editing by each way; Exits; accidental entries; prompts shown, and how each ended (and in B, notices that stayed up for at least a second); links followed and buttons used while looking at the site (and how many through C's box); page switches; saves (and how many through the Exit dialog); the first save, how, and how long after the first unsaved change; Review changes opened and closed without saving (a reload with it open counts as closed); Save clicked while greyed out; unsaved changes now; edits; undos and redos; and Escapes,
- a log of everything, with times since the page was first opened, and **Copy log**,
- 0 | A1 | A2 | B | C links to switch prototype, and **Reset**, which starts again.

The log records every time someone goes into editing (which way, and what was made ready: "Home heading, caret where clicked", "the section … outlined", or nothing) and out again (whether anything was changed in that visit, whether it was saved, and what they chose in the Exit dialog); every click that did nothing; every click on the admin bar, the strip and the sidebar, and every screen opened there ("Strip of icons, Colors: opened the Colors screen", "Tried the admin bar: New"); every other control tried that is only drawn ("Tried View, Tablet", "Tried Document Overview"); every link followed and button used, and which way pages were changed (the menu, the shop's name, a link in the text, the page finder, the arrows, the Pages screen, a link in the footer); every prompt shown and how it ended; every edit, with the page and the old and new words; Escapes, undo and redo; Save clicked while greyed out, Ctrl+S or ⌘S pressed and where, Review changes opened (with what it listed), each Discard in it, Discard all, closed without saving and how, and every save with what it saved and how; reloads; and Finished.

What the counts mean:

- **A click that does nothing** is a click while looking at the site on anything that isn't a link, a button, a box or a box's label, or inside one. In the control it does nothing; in A1, A2 and C it's what starts their own route. A click that ends a drag to select text isn't counted, nor is a touch that stands in for hover in B. A double or triple click counts once, in every arm.
- **A click on the chrome** (`g`) is any click on the admin bar, the strip of icons or the sidebar, whether it opened something, was tried, or landed on an empty part. Clicks on the canvas toolbar (Edit, the page's name, Back and Forward, View) aren't counted here.
- **Prompts** are A2's box, B's notice and C's box. Each one shown ends one way: **accepted** (chose to edit), **dismissed** (No, ×, Escape, Keep looking), **followed** (C only: chose the link, button or box; counted with dismissed in the code) or **ignored** (went away with no choice: the pointer left, a click elsewhere, a scroll, a page change, going into editing another way, a reload). B's notice stays when the page changes or the site scrolls.
- **An accidental entry** is a time into editing that ended with Exit and no change made in it.
- **An unsaved change** is counted as Create not Learn's Review changes lists them: one for the header, one for the footer, one for each page with something changed.

## Running a test

Each prototype opens in a desktop browser with no sign-in. **Use Reset in the notes panel between participants**: reloading keeps everything.

### Who

People who are about to make, or have just made, their first website: a small business, a club, a portfolio. Not developers, and not people who have used WordPress or another site builder's editor before.

### Design

One prototype per participant, since once someone has found the way into editing they know it. Change the order you run them in from day to day.

[Pollock](../pollock/) found that groups of five or six can't separate designs like these. With five arms, use small groups to find problems with the prototypes and the script, and plan for about 20 people per arm for a result, or test fewer arms against the control.

**Don't say "edit", "editing", "save", "change mode", "browse" or "click on it" before they do.** The tasks say what the owner wants done, not how. Don't point at the toolbar or the sidebar.

Start with: "This is the website of a small bookshop, Cobble Lane Books, open in the tool you'd use to change it. Imagine it's your shop and your website. I'll ask you to do a few things with it. Please think aloud as you go. There are no wrong answers; we're testing the design, not you."

### Tasks

1. "Change the main heading on the home page to something of your own."
   *Watch for:* the first thing they do. In the control, do they click the heading, and how many times, before they find Edit? Do they look in the sidebar or the strip first (`g`), and which screens do they open? In A1, the click on the heading goes straight in: is that a surprise, welcome or not? In A2, do they read the question or click No out of habit? In B, the notice is there almost at once, under the Edit button: do they read it, and press its Edit or the ×? Afterwards, do they use the real Edit? In C, the box appears on the first click: which choice?
2. "Make sure people visiting your website can see your new heading."
   *Watch for:* the save task. Do they look for Save, and where? Do they notice it went from grey to blue, or the pulse? What do they make of Review changes, and do they press "Save 1 change" or Cancel? Do they press Ctrl+S, or Exit and find the dialog's Save (`d` in the code)? Some will say it's already done because they can see it: ask how they know visitors see it. In a moderated session, if they move on without saving, note it and don't prompt.
3. "A customer has rung to ask when you're open on Sundays. Find the opening hours on your website."
   *Watch for:* whether they leave editing first (Exit) or look for the hours while still in it; seeing them while editing doesn't tick the task. Which way they get to Contact (the menu, the page finder, the Pages screen). In B, does the notice come back after they've been editing, and does it stop them using the menu (it covers it)?
4. "There's a spelling mistake in the line about opening days at the very bottom of the page. Fix it."
   *Watch for:* how they get back into editing the second time, and whether it's faster than the first. In A1, A2 and C, a click on the footer line is the shortest way. Whether they save without being asked to, now that they have once: the dotted task digits and `w` say what was left unsaved.
5. "Check that the menu at the top of your website works. Try each item."
   *Watch for:* whether they're still in editing, where the menu does nothing, and what they make of that. In A1, clicks on the menu work, but a click beside them goes into editing: count accidental entries. In C, every click asks first: do they choose "Go to …" each time, or get annoyed? In B, the notice covers the menu whenever it shows, and comes back each time they move over the site until they close it: do they close it with the ×, or get stuck? A click aimed at "Join the book club" can land on the notice's Edit or ×: check the recording before counting B's accepted or dismissed, or its first entry, as a choice.
6. "Change the words on the 'See what's on' button to something else."
   *Watch for:* the hard case: the button is a link and its words can be changed. In the control and B, they need to be editing first, then click the words. In A1 and A2, clicking the button goes to the Events page, because it's a link: do they work out how to change it? In C, the box offers "Go to Events" or "Edit this": which do they choose? And again, do they save?

### Questions to close with

- "How did you get from looking at your website to changing it? How did you know?"
- "Was anything confusing or surprising when you clicked on your website?"
- "When you changed the heading, how did you make sure visitors would see it? How did you know it had worked?"
- "Are all your changes on the website now?" (Check the notes panel's unsaved changes before you ask.)
- "Did you use anything on the left of the screen? What did you expect to find there?"
- In A1: "When you clicked on the heading, what happened? Was that what you expected?" And: "Did you ever end up changing the page when you only meant to look?"
- In A2: "A small box asked whether you wanted to edit. Was that useful, or in the way?"
- In B: "Did you notice the message at the top of the page, under the Edit button? What did it tell you?" If yes: "Did it get in the way at any point?" And: "If you wanted to change something tomorrow, where would you click?"
- In C: "Every click gave you two choices. How did that feel after a while?"
- In the control: "If you'd clicked on the heading and it had let you change it straight away, would that have been better or worse?"

### What to watch for

- **Time to first editing, and the way in.** The `f` part. This is the main measure: compare each arm with the control, and check that the arm's own way (`a`) is the one people used. If most people in an arm still went in by the Edit button, the arm's own way didn't help, whatever the time.
- **Clicks that did nothing before.** The `n` part. In the control this is the wasted effort the arms are meant to remove. In A1, A2 and C the click that starts the arm's own route is one of them, so `n1` is the least there when that's how someone went in; more means clicks whose question they turned down (No or ignored in A2, Keep looking in C). In A1 every such click goes in, so it's always `n1` there unless they went in by the Edit button first.
- **Looking in the wrong place.** The `g` part: clicks on the admin bar, the strip and the sidebar before going in. Many in the control and few in an arm means the arm caught people before they went looking elsewhere.
- **Finding Save.** Task 2's digit, and the `v` part: how long from the first change to the first save, and how. `t` means Save was found; `k` means they knew the shortcut; `d` means they saved only because Exit asked, so Save itself wasn't found. A dotted task part and `w` above 0 mean they finished with changes unsaved. Save is the same in every arm, so this mostly describes Create not Learn's Save, not the arms; an arm that changes it (people in B going in by the notice's Edit, say, then never looking at the toolbar) is worth noting.
- **Accidental entries.** The `x` part. The cost of A1 especially: every click beside a link while browsing goes into editing.
- **Prompts dismissed and ignored.** The `p` part. A prompt that's mostly dismissed or ignored is noise. In B, read only accepted and dismissed, with "seen" from the notes panel: its shown and ignored rise and fall with how the pointer happens to cross the site.
- **Getting the browse tasks done.** Tasks 3 and 5, and the `b` part. An arm that gets people into editing faster but leaves them stuck there, or keeps pulling them back in, is worse.
- **The words.** Note what people call looking, changing and saving ("view mode", "the live site", "editing", "publish", "the back end") and whether the arms' words ("Edit this", "Keep looking") and Save's ("Save 1 change", "Visitors see these once you save them") match them.

### What would favour each

Each arm's cost has two sides: what it does to people while they're only looking at their site, and what it would take to build and keep in the real editor.

- **A1 (click to edit)**: the shortest time to first editing (`f…a`), `n` near 1, few clicks elsewhere (`g`), and no more accidental entries (`x`) or failed browse tasks than the control. It counts against A1 if people land in editing by surprise while trying to look (tasks 3 and 5), or if task 6 goes worse because the button follows its link. It's the cheapest to explain but changes what a click on your own site means, which a real editor would have to make clear everywhere the site is shown.
- **A2 (ask on click)**: nearly A1's speed, with fewer accidental entries, and most prompts accepted the first time. It counts against A2 if people click No without reading, or find the question tiresome during the browse tasks (many dismissed). Cheap to build; the cost is an extra step every time.
- **B (hover notice)**: people read the notice, press its Edit, and later use the real Edit in the same place; the × keeps it away while they browse. Showing at once, it can't be missed, so a good result is mostly accepted with few dismissed. It counts against B if it's dismissed or ignored by most people (many ×), if covering the menu holds up tasks 3 and 5, or if people still click around the site first (`n` like the control). The second time into editing says whether it taught the Edit button: `b` above 0 and later entries by the Edit button (in the notes) are the sign. Cheap to build; on touch screens it relies on the first-touch rule.
- **C (choose on click)**: people choose Edit this on the first click and land where they wanted, task 6 included. It counts against C if the box on every link and button slows the browse tasks (`l` with many via the box) or annoys people, or if they choose "Keep looking" and then don't know where to go. The most intrusive while browsing, and the most work to build well, since every link, button and box on the site has to be wrapped.
- **The control**: people find the Edit button as quickly as the others get in, with few clicks that do nothing and few on the chrome. Then the button is enough, and none of the others is worth building.
- **Save, in all five**: most people save the heading from the toolbar (`v…t`) within a short time of changing it, and finish with nothing unsaved (`w0`). It counts against Create not Learn's Save if many save only from the Exit dialog (`d`), close Review changes without saving, or finish with changes unsaved. Since Create not Learn's own Exit discards without asking, a `d` there would have been lost work.

Some of these could be combined. If B helps people see that there's a way in and A2 or C helps them take it, a next round can put the two together.

## Running it

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step and nothing loaded from elsewhere: `app.js` and `style.css` are the shared site and the editor around it, and each prototype page sets `data-variant` on `<body>` (`control`, `click-to-edit`, `ask-on-click`, `hover-notice` or `choose-on-click`). The control is `app.js` and `style.css` alone; each of the other four also loads its own script and stylesheet from `variants/`. The arms' names and code prefixes are in the `VARIANTS` table in `app.js`, so a code is right even if an arm's script fails. The arms use only the small API `app.js` exposes as `window.Vermeer`, documented at the top of `app.js`: what was clicked and whether it's a link, button or box; going into editing by a named way, with the caret at a point; doing what a click would have done; recording prompts and their outcomes; and a little state of their own that survives a reload. Everything is kept in `localStorage` under `vermeer:<variant>`. Serve the repository root with any static server and open `vermeer/`.

`node vermeer/e2e/smoke.mjs` runs every prototype in headless Chrome with Playwright (set `NODE_PATH` to where Playwright is installed if it's installed globally). It starts its own server, checks that each prototype loads without errors with the chrome around it, does all six tasks in the control by the Edit button (with clicks on the strip, the sidebar's Pages screen and the admin bar on the way, and saves through Review changes and the keyboard) and checks the ticks, the counts and the code; checks the save parts of the code with changes left unsaved and saved from the Exit dialog; checks that a reload puts everything back (looking at the site, and editing with an unsaved change), the Exit dialog's three choices, Reset, the API the arms use, and the layout at 1024×768. It then runs `vermeer/e2e/<arm>.mjs` for each arm that has one, which test that arm's own way in, its prompt outcomes, its code parts and its keyboard route.

The bookshop, its address and phone number, its events, books and people are placeholders.
